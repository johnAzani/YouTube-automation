## 1. How the 2026 algorithm actually ranks

Three model changes matter more than everything else combined:

1. **Satisfaction beats raw watch time.** YouTube feeds 1–5★ viewer-satisfaction surveys
   directly into ranking. A 4-minute video viewers *loved* outranks an 8-minute video they
   merely tolerated. Watch time is now weighted by satisfaction → *"watch time + satisfaction
   = session contribution."*
2. **Session contribution is the leading long-form signal.** Does your video extend the
   viewer's YouTube session (they watch 2 more videos after) or end it (they close the app)?
   Videos that end sessions get fewer Suggested placements. **Implication: playlists, series,
   end screens and "watch next" verbal CTAs are ranking features, not decoration.**
3. **Shorts and long-form algorithms are formally separated** (2026). Flop Shorts no longer
   drag down long-form — so Shorts volume is a free option on subs.

### The test window (every video, every channel — new channels included)

```
publish →  seed test (subscribers + ~10–100 lookalike viewers)
        →  measure CTR + first-minute retention + satisfaction  (decisive window: 24h, some niches <24h)
        →  expand to Browse/Suggested over days 2–14 if signals hold  (or shrink and freeze)
```
- ~70% of a video's lifetime trajectory is decided in the first 2–24 hours.
- First **60 seconds carry ~3× weight**; a steep drop at 0:30 kills the test.
- Videos can be re-tested weeks later if demand changes — never delete, keep the library clean.

### Traffic-mix targets (channel health)

| Source | Typical channel | Target for a resilient channel | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Browse + Suggested | ~65% | 50–60% | Growth engine but outage/update-fragile (Feb 2026 outage wiped algo-dependent channels) |
| YouTube Search | ~20% | **25–35%** | Outage-proof, compounds for years, matches documentary intent |
| Subs + external | ~15% | 15–20% | Returning viewers are worth **5–10× more** than new viewers |

CTR benchmarks by surface: Browse 3–8% · Search 5–15% · Suggested 2–5%.

## 2. The first-48-hours ritual (do this for every upload)

| When | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| T−2–4h | Schedule live 2–4 h before your audience peak (Analytics → Audience → *When your viewers are on YouTube*) | +15–25% first-24h views |
| T+0 | Pinned comment (question to answer) | Comment velocity signal |
| T+0 | Community post pointing at the video | Seed the subscriber test |
| T+0 | End screen → next video in the series + playlist link | Session contribution |
| T+0 | Chapters in description (already generated by `pipeline/metadata.py`) | Key-moments + search |
| T+0–1h | Reply to every early comment | Satisfaction + loyalty |
| **Don't** | Change title/thumbnail in the first 24 h | Resets the packaging test |
| **Don't** | Publish another video within 72 h (unless a planned sequel) | Let the test finish |
| T+24–48h | Check retention graph; if steep 0:30 drop → edit hook for the *next* video (not this one's metadata) | Learning loop |

## 3. Retention editing rules (what the script/scene layer must respect)

1. **Hook contract (first 30 s):** cold open with the most surprising fact/stake — no intros,
   no logos, no "welcome back." Promise a specific payoff, then tease it.
2. **Open loops:** at 0:15 tease the 4:30 payoff; at 4:30 tease the 8:00 one. Close each loop
   before opening the next. End by **calling back to the intro line** ("memory loops" get
   quoted in comments → return rate).
3. **Pattern interrupt every 60–90 s:** visual change (b-roll swap, map, archive photo, zoom),
   text overlay, tone shift, question. Monotony causes subconscious drop-off even when the
   content is strong. Our scene-per-heading structure does this mechanically — keep scenes
   ≤ ~90 s of narration each.
4. **Payoff before average view duration:** put the strongest reveal slightly *before* the
   channel's typical AVD, then escalate — never save everything for the end.
5. **Chapter every 1–2 min**, keyword-named, first starts at 0:00 (valid chapters: ≥3, ≥10 s).
6. **Session CTA:** last 20 s = "the full story of X is here" (end screen) — not a subscribe beg.

## 4. Packaging science (titles + thumbnails)

### 4.1 Title spec

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Hard max | 100 chars |
| Visible before truncation | ~60–70 desktop · **~45–55 mobile** |
| Sweet spot | 55–65 chars, primary keyword in **first 5 words** |
| Formula | [Keyword/topic]: [hook] — numbers, brackets, contrast, warning, specific outcome |

Examples (history niche): "The Canal That Broke a City: America's Forgotten Flood" ·
"Why Rome Didn't Fall (What Actually Happened)" · "The $2B Mistake Nobody Was Punished For".
Every word earns its place; cut filler ("a/the/an") when tight.

### 4.2 Thumbnail science

- vidIQ's 500-breakout-video study: **69% used a face** (80% of biggest overperformers);
  89% used a face **or** high contrast. Faceless documentary still gets faces — archive
  portraits, crowds, statues, maps-with-figures. Emotion (surprise/stakes) beats pretty.
- **3–5 words bold text max**, readable at the **150×83 px preview test** (scale it down —
  if unreadable, it's too small/busy). High contrast subject/background; one idea only.
- Click-promise test before you design: *"This video shows [audience] why/how [outcome] by
  revealing [mechanism]."* If the image + title don't jointly deliver that promise, redesign.

### 4.3 Test & Compare SOP (native A/B — free, use on every long-form)

YouTube Studio → video → **A/B test titles & thumbnails**: up to 3 variants (thumbnails,
titles, or combined). Critical fine print:

- **Winner is chosen by watch-time share per impression — NOT raw CTR.** A clickbait variant
  can win clicks and lose the test. Good — that keeps quality honest.
- Budget: **≥ 2,000–5,000 impressions per variant** (≈1–2 weeks). 0.5pp CTR differences need
  ~25k impressions/variant — don't chase noise. Verdicts: *Winner / Performed Same /
  Inconclusive* — "Inconclusive" is a valid answer; keep variant 1.
- Test **distinct** variants (different color/subject/text), one element class at a time.
  Don't stop early. Document every test (wins teach the next design).
- Requires Advanced features (phone + ID verification); desktop-only; long-form only
  (not Shorts/Premieres/Made-for-Kids). The pipeline generates `*_thumb_a/b/c.jpg` drafts —
  swap backgrounds/colors and feed all three into Test & Compare at publish.

## 5. Metadata spec sheet (2026 hard limits)

| Field | Hard limit | The number that matters | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | 100 chars | ~55–65 visible | Keyword first 5 words; hook included |
| Description | 5,000 chars (bytes) | **first 150–157 chars** = "money zone" | Keyword + promise above the fold; 200–300 word body |
| Chapters | — | 3–8 timestamps | Start 0:00, ≥10 s each, 1/1–2 min, keyword-named |
| Tags | 500 chars total | low weight (NLP reads transcript) | 5–15; exact target keyword first |
| Hashtags | **15 max** (16+ → ALL ignored) | first 3 shown above title | 3–5, include `#Shorts` on shorts |
| Shorts title | 100 chars | ~40 in shelf tile | Hook in first 3 words |
| Channel keywords | 500 chars | 5–10 phrases | Set once in Studio |
| Caption track | — | accuracy | Upload our SRT; mis-transcribed terms miss search |

Description anatomy (built automatically by `pipeline/metadata.py`):
`money zone (hook + keyword)` → 2–3 sentence summary → chapters → sources/links → hashtags.

## 6. Idea validation rubric (score before you write)

Score each idea 1–5; **write only ideas scoring ≥ 18/25**:

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search demand | No one queries this | Some long-tail queries | Clear recurring query volume |
| Competition gap | 5+ dominant videos | Some weak coverage | Thin/poor coverage (old, boring, wrong) |
| Retention potential | Encyclopedia dump | Standard narrative | Built-in mystery/stakes/twist |
| Visual availability | No archive/footage | Generic stock only | Rich archive/maps/photographs |
| RPM adjacency | Pure entertainment | Educational | Business/science/tech-adjacent (non-advisory) |

Anti-fragile mix per 10 videos: 4 search-demand plays (compounders) + 4 browse/suggested
plays (curiosity/narrative) + 2 experiments. Kill a format after 10 consecutive misses.

## 7. Shorts playbook (the sub engine)

- **22–45 s is the sweet spot** (50–60 s Shorts pull ~22× the views of <10 s; sub-15 s lost
  reach in 2026). Push gates: ~65% AVD (<30 s) · ~50% AVD (30–60 s).
- Cut from proven long-form scenes (`pipeline/shorts.py` does scene-boundary cuts). Cold-open
  hook text in frame 1; loopable ending (last line feeds the first).
- 4–7/week, 7–11 AM target-audience time. `#Shorts` + 2 hashtags in description.
- Clip views are worth $0.03–0.10 RPM — they buy **subs and brand recall**, nothing else.
  Every Short's description links the parent long-form.

## 8. Multi-language — the free 2026 growth lever

- **YouTube auto-dubbing opened to ALL eligible creators on 4 Feb 2026** (27 languages;
  free; Gemini-powered; Studio → Settings → Content → Automatic dubbing). 80% of YouTube
  views come from outside the US — auto-dub expands reach at ₦0 with zero production cost.
- Caveats: auto-dubs carry an "Auto-dubbed" label, don't translate titles/descriptions
  (no foreign SEO), quality is uneven, and you can't edit tracks post-publish.
- Upgrade path (later, when revenue exists): custom multi-language audio tracks
  (AI dubbing ~$1–10/min) unlock localized titles = foreign-language SEO.
- Strategy order: (1) win English US/UK/CA/AU first — that's where the RPM is; (2) turn on
  auto-dubbing from month 2; (3) buy custom dubs only for proven hits.

## 9. Audio & music rules (2026)

- AI-generated **music** is monetizable **only with disclosure** (Studio → "Altered or
  synthetic content" toggle). Non-disclosure alone can trigger demonetization; disclosure
  costs nothing and doesn't reduce reach.
- Pure AI-music/formulaic-audio channels are the #1 inauthentic-content enforcement target
  (est. 40%+ of them hit since late 2025). As *background* music under human-written
  documentary narration, AI music is fine — with the toggle, keeping generation records.
- Content ID matches registered reference audio (it is not an "AI detector"); AI tracks are
  mostly unregistered today but that can change via ongoing lawsuits — prefer YouTube Audio
  Library / CC0 (Pixabay Music) when possible (`MUSIC_DIR` in this pipeline).
- Never clone a real person's voice. Your own cloned voice = fine.
