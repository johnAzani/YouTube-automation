## 1. The correction I have to make first

My earlier notes used finance RPM figures of roughly **$6–$15 per 1,000 views** for planning. Those numbers came from SEO and creator-tool articles. After finding actual Studio data, I think they were too generous, and I want to correct that before we commit to anything.

**AIR Media-Tech published Studio data from 300 monetized channels, 3,595 channel-months, May 2025 – May 2026:**

| Finding | Number |
|---|---:|
| Median RPM across all 300 channels | **$2.30** per 1,000 views |
| Median share of views that showed an ad | **53%** |
| Business & Finance median RPM (only 3 channels sampled — directional) | **$2.01** |
| Education & Science median RPM (highest niche) | **$10.22** |
| Education & Science top quartile / bottom quartile | **$19.50 / $2.31** |
| Share of all ad revenue earned by the top 10% of channels | **58%** |
| Median RPM, small channels (10K–100K subs) vs medium (100K–10M) | **$2.14 vs $2.04** |

The last row is the one that matters most: **subscriber count barely changes what a view pays.** The niche sets a range; where you land inside it depends on audience country, how many views carry an ad, and how clean your monetization setup is.

This does not mean the $10–$25 finance RPM claims are fabricated. They are usually **advertiser CPM**, before YouTube's 45% cut and before unmonetized views are counted. A $25 CPM with 53% of views monetized is roughly $7 RPM — and lower again if the audience is outside Tier 1 countries.

**The planning consequence:** plan on **$2–$8 RPM** until your own Studio data says otherwise, not $12–$25. At $2.30, one million views is about **$2,300**, not $12,000.

The AIR sample has a caveat: it is 300 channels that an agency works with, which may skew toward lower-RPM geographies. It is not a census. But it is real Studio data, and it is more credible than a calculator widget.

## 2. The base rates I did not have before

This is the part that should shape the plan more than any niche choice.

**vidIQ, July 2026 — 61.2 million channels with at least one public subscriber:**

| Milestone | Channels at or above | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1,000 subscribers | 24,813,187 | 40.6% |
| 10,000 | 4,836,743 | 7.9% |
| 100,000 | 792,376 | 1.3% |
| 1,000,000 | 79,570 | 0.13% |

The median channel in that sample had **508 subscribers**.

**vidIQ, 9,414 channels created after 2023 that crossed 1,000 subscribers:**

- Median time to 1,000: **about 16 months**
- **68% needed more than 40 videos**
- The single most common path was **150 or more videos (31%)**

**vidIQ, more than 1 million channels at the 1,000-subscriber mark:**

- **89% get under 5,000 monthly views.**

**And at 5,000 views per month and a $2.30 RPM, that is about $11.50 per month.**

That is the honest picture. Monetization is not the finish line; it is the starting line, and most people who reach it earn almost nothing.

**What this means for the "three videos in six weeks" plan:** it is a **format test**, not a path to income. Three videos will not reach 1,000 subscribers, and they are not supposed to. Their job is to tell us whether we can produce this format sustainably and whether it retains. If we confuse the pilot with the business, we will quit at month four for the wrong reason — which is exactly when most faceless channels quit.

## 3. Where the money actually is

If ads pay roughly $2–$8 per 1,000 views, then a channel needs enormous reach before ads matter:

| Monthly views | At $2.30 RPM | At $5 RPM | At $10 RPM |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | $23 | $50 | $100 |
| 50,000 | $115 | $250 | $500 |
| 100,000 | $230 | $500 | $1,000 |
| 500,000 | $1,150 | $2,500 | $5,000 |
| 1,000,000 | $2,300 | $5,000 | $10,000 |

**Ads are a scale business, and we are not at scale.** So the question is not "which niche has the highest RPM." It is "what else can this audience pay for?"

### 3.1 Sponsorship

Finance sponsorships are genuinely more valuable than ads, because fintech companies pay for customer acquisition, not impressions.

| Source | Finance sponsorship CPM | What it implies |
|---|---:|---|
| OutlierKit (2026) | $30–$60 | 50K views ≈ $1,500–$3,000 |
| SponsorRadar (2026) | $40–$80 | 50K views ≈ $2,000–$4,000 |
| InfluenceFlow (2026) | $25–$65 | 50K views ≈ $1,250–$3,250 |

But the thresholds matter:

- **Nano (1K–10K subs):** $50–$500 per video, often product-only.
- **Micro (10K–100K):** $200–$5,000; finance-specific estimates at 10K subs run **$1,200–$2,500** per video when the audience matches.
- **100K+:** brands start cold-outreach.

And a caution: Aspire's 2026 survey of roughly 900 marketers and creators found the **average sponsorship CPM fell 42% year on year to $2.68**. Finance is an outlier, but the direction of the market is down, not up.

**Realistic read:** sponsorship becomes meaningful around **10,000 subscribers with a Tier-1 audience**, and becomes a real business around **50,000–100,000**.

### 3.2 Products

This is the part I under-weighted before.

Caleb Hammer's company gets **nearly half its revenue from app subscriptions, memberships and courses**, not ads. His app has about 26,000 paying subscribers and is on track for **more than $3.2 million this year**. That is the clearest evidence in the whole research set: **the audience is worth more than the views.**

A product does not need 100,000 subscribers. It needs a few hundred people with a specific, painful problem:

| Product price | Buyers/month | Monthly revenue |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $9 | 50 | $450 |
| $19 | 50 | $950 |
| $29 | 100 | $2,900 |
| $49 | 100 | $4,900 |
| $99 | 50 | $4,950 |

Compare that with the views needed for the same money at a $2.30 RPM:

| Monthly revenue | Views needed at $2.30 RPM |
|---:|---:|
| $450 | ~196,000 |
| $950 | ~413,000 |
| $2,900 | ~1.26 million |
| $4,900 | ~2.13 million |

**Selling 50 workbooks at $19 is easier than getting 400,000 views.** That is the strategic insight I want us to act on.

### 3.3 Affiliate

Finance affiliate income is real but concentrated in the US and UK: credit cards, brokerages, robo-advisors, business accounts. It generally opens up around **50,000 subscribers**, because that is when click volume becomes meaningful. It is a year-two or year-three line, not a launch strategy.

### 3.4 Services

The fastest cash is not the channel at all: it is using the same skills to make finance explainers, research, or calculations for fintech companies. That is a services business, not automation, and it competes for the same hours as the channel. Worth naming, not worth confusing with the plan.

## 4. Three coherent strategies

Each is internally consistent. They are not variations of the same plan.

### Strategy A — The media company

Build a faceless explainer with broad reach. Monetize with ads and sponsorships at scale.

- **Benchmark:** How Money Works (1.75M subs, 327 videos, ~4.2M views/month); Economics Explained (2.89M subs, team-owned).
- **Requires:** 100K+ subs, 1M+ views/month, 2–4 years, consistent quality, probably a team.
- **Revenue at scale:** $2,300–$10,000/month from ads at 1M views; plus sponsorships.
- **Probability for a solo, ₦0 start:** low.
- **Fit with your constraints:** poor.
- **Honest verdict:** highest ceiling, lowest probability, slowest cash. This is the plan most "YouTube automation" content sells you, and it is the plan most likely to burn a year.

### Strategy B — Narrow authority plus product

Own a specific money problem for a specific audience. Monetize with a paid tool or workbook, then sponsorships, then ads.

- **Benchmark:** the finance-product economics above; Caleb Hammer's app; nano/micro sponsorship rates.
- **Requires:** 2,000–20,000 engaged subscribers, a genuinely painful problem, one well-made product, 9–18 months.
- **Revenue:** $500–$5,000/month from 50–200 product sales, before sponsors.
- **Probability:** moderate — higher than A, because it needs fewer views and the product can iterate.
- **Fit with your constraints:** good. The repo already builds calculations and charts, which is exactly what a paid model or workbook is.
- **Honest verdict:** lower ceiling, much higher probability, faster cash, but product-market risk replaces audience-scale risk.

### Strategy C — Nigeria-first practical money

Build a local audience around naira reality. Monetize with Nigerian fintech sponsors, products priced in naira, and services.

- **Benchmark:** 100+ Nigerian channels have passed 1M subscribers; Nigerian RPM estimates cluster at **$0.66–$3**, with finance-specific estimates at **$2.50–$5.60**.
- **Requires:** local relevance, business relationships with Nigerian fintechs, naira-priced products, 12–24 months.
- **Revenue:** ad revenue is close to irrelevant at small scale. A channel with 100,000 monthly views and a purely Nigerian audience is estimated at roughly **$50–$200/month**. Sponsorships and products carry the business.
- **Probability:** moderate on audience, uncertain on monetization.
- **Fit with your constraints:** highest authenticity, lowest competition, weakest ad economics.
- **Honest verdict:** defensible and locally meaningful, but it is a sponsorship-and-product business wearing a YouTube costume.

### The comparison

| | A: Media company | B: Authority + product | C: Nigeria-first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Views needed for $2,000/month | ~1M/month + sponsors | ~100 product sales | sponsors/products |
| Time to first meaningful revenue | 24–48 months | 9–18 months | 12–24 months |
| Ad revenue at small scale | negligible | negligible | negligible |
| Competition | very high | moderate | low–moderate |
| Your authenticity advantage | low | moderate | high |
| Automation fit | medium | high | medium |
| Ceiling | very high | moderate | moderate |
| Main risk | never reaches scale | product doesn't sell | audience can't pay |

## 5. What I recommend

**Strategy B, pointed at a Tier-1 English audience, with A as an emergent upside and C as a later expansion.**

Concretely:

> A math-forward money explainer for US/UK/global viewers, where every video works through a real calculation with visible assumptions, and the paid product is the model itself — the spreadsheet or tool that lets a viewer run their own numbers.

Why this and not the others:

1. **It matches the evidence on where money actually comes from.** Ads are a scale business; products are a trust business. We can earn trust with 2,000 subscribers. We cannot earn scale with 20 videos.
2. **It uses the studio's real advantage.** The repository already produces calculations, charts and renders. A paid model is a natural extension of that pipeline, not a new business.
3. **It solves the differentiation problem.** "Finance explained" is saturated. "Here is the actual model, with the assumptions visible, and you can have it" is a narrower and more defensible promise.
4. **It is no-regret.** If the channel fails, the models are still sellable, reusable, or portfolio pieces. If the product fails, the videos still build an audience. The two halves hedge each other.
5. **It keeps the honest ceiling visible.** This is not a path to "insane money" in year one. It is a path to $500–$5,000/month in year two if both halves work, with a small chance of much more.

### What I am not recommending

- **Not** a dry money-mechanics channel. The demand evidence in the companion note stands: definitions get 100K–340K lifetime views; stakes get 400K–10M.
- **Not** an AI finance persona. YouTube's policy says channels using AI personas presenting as human financial experts **will not be allowed to monetize**.
- **Not** a high-volume template channel. In January 2026, according to press coverage, YouTube terminated 11 channels and wiped content from others — 16 channels in total, with about 35 million combined subscribers and 4.7 billion lifetime views — under the inauthentic-content policy. Detection uses proxies including upload frequency, format similarity and **absence of on-camera human presence**, so an anonymous template operation is the highest-risk configuration.
- **Not** a plan that depends on ads. At $2–$8 RPM, ads are a bonus, not the business.

### The format, precisely

- **12–18 minutes**, one question, one calculation, one chart.
- **A named human narrator.** Not necessarily on camera, but a real identity, a real voice, and a visible method. This is the strongest defense against both the AI-persona rule and the "this is slop" reaction.
- **Stakes first.** "Why your car costs $650,000" not "what is depreciation."
- **Assumptions on screen.** Every number traceable to a source and a date.
- **One product per pillar, not per video.** Start with one model; test whether anyone wants it before building a second.

### The first three videos

Keep the demand-validated topics:

1. **Why Your Car Is Costing You $650,000** — 472K views on a 20-minute car-cost video; 623K on a Short.
2. **Why Your Rent Keeps Going Up** — 839K and 410K on recent videos; 3.3M Short.
3. **How Banks Make Money From Money They Create** — 10M Kurzgesagt; 473K finance explainer; 1.6M+ Shorts.

And one product hypothesis to test alongside them:

> **A car-cost model** — a spreadsheet where a viewer enters purchase price, loan terms, insurance, fuel and years of ownership, and sees total cost and cost per mile. Priced at $15–$25.

Why car cost first: it has the strongest measured demand, it is universally relevant, it is calculation-heavy, and the existing model has an obvious buyer.

## 6. The decision questions

These are the things only you can answer. They change the plan materially.

### Q1. What is this channel for?

- **(a) Ad income** — weakest and slowest; I would not choose this.
- **(b) Sponsorship income** — needs 10K+ subscribers and a Tier-1 audience; 12–24 months.
- **(c) Product income** — needs 2K–20K subscribers and one good product; 9–18 months.
- **(d) A portfolio and credibility asset** — not primarily income, but real option value for freelance work, a job, or a future business.

My recommendation is **(c)**, with (b) as the second leg and (d) as a genuine benefit.

### Q2. Which audience?

- **(a) US/UK/global English** — highest revenue per view, hardest competition, highest accuracy burden.
- **(b) Nigeria-first** — lowest revenue per view, least competition, most authentic.
- **(c) Diaspora bridge** — Nigerians at home and abroad. I checked this and I have to be honest: the demand evidence is weak. Japa and cost-of-living videos in my search had 8.6K, 1.5K, 5.9K, 330 and 244 views; the money-transfer videos had 31K, 21K, 703 and 395. There is no finance breakout in that space yet. It is either an opening or a warning, and the evidence does not tell us which.

My recommendation is **(a)**, because it is the only lane with measured demand and revenue per view. It is also the hardest, and I want that on the record.

### Q3. How long before you need it to pay?

- Under 6 months: YouTube is the wrong vehicle. Services or a product sold directly would pay faster.
- 6–18 months: Strategy B is viable.
- 18+ months: all three are viable; A becomes more rational.

This is the question that decides everything, and I cannot answer it for you.

### Q4. Voice and identity?

- Named human narrator (my recommendation)
- Real voice, no face
- Anonymous synthetic voice (highest policy and trust risk)

## 7. If we commit, the first 90 days

**Weeks 1–2 — product before video.**
Build the car-cost model. If we cannot make a model worth paying for, we should not make a video about it. This inverts the usual order and it is the most important change from the previous plan.

**Weeks 3–4 — the demand gate and the script.**
Score the topic against the 12-point gate; script the car video around the model; record a 150-word hook and test it before full production.

**Weeks 5–8 — video one, and a payment test.**
Publish video one with a waitlist or a pre-order for the model. The signal we want is not views; it is **whether anyone tries to buy**.

**Weeks 9–12 — videos two and three, plus a decision.**
If the waitlist converts, build the product properly. If it does not, keep publishing and fix the offer before building anything else.

**Stop rules stay as written:** 18 human-hours for the first three videos, 8 hours per video ceiling, no new spending, and a hard review after three videos.

## 8. What would make me change this recommendation

- **The car model gets no pre-orders after three videos.** The product thesis is wrong; either change the product or fall back to Strategy A and accept the slower ad path.
- **Retention is below 35%.** The format is not working; fix the hook and pacing before adding topics.
- **Production exceeds 8 hours per video.** The format is too ambitious; cut to 8–10 minutes and one chart.
- **The audience turns out to be overwhelmingly Nigerian.** Then Strategy C is the real plan, and the product needs naira pricing and local sponsors.
- **YouTube rejects the channel as inauthentic.** Stop and audit before resubmitting. Do not reupload the same thing.

## 9. The one-paragraph version

Finance demand is real, but the "finance pays the most" story is mostly a CPM story, not an RPM story: real Studio data from 300 channels shows a $2.30 median RPM and $2.01 for business and finance. Reaching 1,000 subscribers takes a median of 16 months and 40–150 videos, and 89% of channels that get there still earn under 5,000 views a month. So ads cannot be the business. The money is in sponsorship and products, which need trust rather than scale. The best fit for this studio is a math-forward money explainer for a Tier-1 audience, with a paid calculation model as the product and the videos as the proof. That is a 9–18 month path to $500–$5,000 a month if both halves work, not a path to instant wealth, and it is designed so that failure still leaves usable assets.
