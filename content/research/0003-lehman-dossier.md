# Lehman Brothers: research dossier for documentary 0003

**Research date: 23 October 2026. Scope: the checked-in eight-scene Lehman draft, plus the accounting, rescue and recovery details needed to assess its thesis.**

**Editorial status: machine-assisted research, not human authorship or publishing approval.** No human-review flag is changed by this document. This is not financial or legal advice.

## Executive finding

The draft's strongest subject is not a secret transaction that single-handedly caused the financial crisis. It is the relationship between **reported financial strength, actual asset risk and immediately available funding**. The documents support a story about a highly leveraged institution exposed to real estate, dependent on continued market funding, using temporary reporting-period transactions to make a closely watched leverage measure look better. They do not establish that lenders discovered Repo 105 during the final weekend and then withdrew because of that discovery. The examiner's detailed public account arrived in 2010, after the 2008 failure. [S03, S04, S05, S19]

Recommended title: **How Lehman Brothers Failed: Repo 105, Leverage and the Funding Run**.

Recommended thesis: **A cleaner-looking quarter-end balance sheet was not a durable solution to Lehman's asset and funding problems.** This is an editorial synthesis of the evidence, not a quotation or a judicial finding.

The earlier draft contains a reversed legal-opinion claim, incorrect AIG chronology, an incomplete accounting explanation, overbroad recovery language and an unsupported causal ending. These should be corrected before human sign-off. The separate [claim audit](0003-lehman-claim-audit.md) explains the disposition of the original narration.

## 1. Evidence standard and research limits

### What counts as evidence here

- **Primary records:** Lehman SEC filings; the court-appointed examiner's report; fund notices preserved as SEC court exhibits; Federal Reserve/Treasury announcements; SIPC's liquidation account; administrators' final report.
- **Official inquiry:** the Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission (FCIC), including its reproduction of witness accounts and its own conclusions. A commission conclusion is not a criminal verdict.
- **Participant testimony:** New York Fed general counsel Thomas Baxter's explanation. It is important first-hand evidence but also the institution's defense of its decisions, not a neutral adjudication of every contested fact.
- **Institutional research:** Yale case studies and New York Fed researchers' recovery analysis. These provide structured synthesis and useful calculations, but are not substitutes for primary records when the details conflict.
- **Contemporaneous reporting:** used narrowly where direct source access failed, with that limitation stated.

### Extent actually examined

The source register at the end states which portions were examined. In particular:

- All eight returned chunks of the FCIC's standalone Chapter 18 and all eight chunks of Yale's Repo 105 case study were read.
- Only the accessible **opening portion** of examiner Volume 3 was read. The PDF retrieval is limited to its opening 30 pages, including cover/contents. Its table of contents identifies later sections, but that is **not** evidence that those later hundreds of pages were read.
- The entire nine-volume examiner report, underlying interviews, all transaction confirmations, the full Linklaters opinion, and the complete litigation docket were **not** independently audited.
- Large SEC filings were examined in selected portions and targeted search excerpts, not read end to end. Those access levels are recorded below.
- This pass does not certify the latest balance of every Lehman estate on 23 October 2026. Recovery examples have explicit historical cutoffs.
- Neither a source's institutional prestige nor a search result's displayed date removes the need to check the actual document's date, entity and definition.

No claims in this dossier depend on an invented primary-document reading. Sources are linked rather than bulk copied. Short labels and necessary terminology are used; the report otherwise paraphrases.

## 2. Entity map: there was no single legal 'Lehman'

| Name | Role relevant to this story | Why it must not be conflated |
|---|---|---|
| Lehman Brothers Holdings Inc. (**LBHI**) | U.S. parent/holding company; Chapter 11 debtor from 15 September 2008 | Parent bondholders and creditors do not automatically share a broker-dealer customer's rights or recoveries. |
| Lehman Brothers Inc. (**LBI**) | U.S. broker-dealer; separate SIPA liquidation beginning 19 September 2008 | Customer property and general unsecured claims have different treatment. Its 2022 case closure is not the closure of every Lehman entity. |
| Lehman Brothers International (Europe) (**LBIE**) | European broker-dealer; English-law transaction route; UK administration | Its administration, client property, house estate and later solvent winding-down process are distinct from LBHI Chapter 11. |
| Lehman Brothers Special Financing (**LBSF**) | Derivatives affiliate | Its creditors' distributions cannot be generalized to LBHI or LBI creditors. |
| Other affiliates | Separate operations and cross-border proceedings | Common branding and consolidated reporting do not erase legal entity boundaries. |

Evidence: original bankruptcy-related 8-K [S20], asset-sale 8-K [S11], New York Fed recovery analysis [S13], SIPC [S14], and LBIE administrators [S15].

**Editorial consequence:** specify the parent when narrating the September 15 filing; specify the U.S. broker-dealer when discussing 2022 recoveries; never write that 'all of Lehman finally closed in 2022.'

## 3. Founding, age and corporate identity

Harvard Baker Library's archival timeline separates **Henry Lehman's 1844 dry-goods store** in Montgomery, Alabama, from the **1847–1850 arrival of his brothers and development of Lehman Brothers**. It dates the New York branch to 1858 and the American Express spin-off to 1994. [S01]

This explains the competing age labels: 2008 minus 1850 gives 158; 2008 minus 1844 gives 164. They use different starting points, not necessarily different arithmetic. The original narration incorrectly compresses the store's beginning and the three-brother business into one 1850 founding event. Yale's overview itself compresses the early history; prefer the archival timeline for the staged arrival. [S01, S19]

**Safe narration:** 'Its roots reached back to a dry-goods store opened by Henry Lehman in Alabama in eighteen forty-four. His brothers later joined him, and the enterprise became Lehman Brothers.' The documentary need not spend airtime arguing over whether to call it a 158- or 164-year-old firm.

The 1994 transaction should be called a **spin-off from American Express**, not casually an IPO that first created the business. A contemporaneous June 1, 1994 New York Times search excerpt describes completion of a distribution of shares to American Express shareholders. The direct article fetch failed, so the precise completion-date detail is not promoted into the revised narration. Harvard's timeline supports the year and Richard Fuld's leadership. [S01, S18]

'Nobody believed Lehman could fail' is demonstrably too strong. FCIC describes concern after Bear Stearns, market pricing of Lehman's credit risk, critical public analysis, regulators' stress work and counterparties reducing exposures before the final weekend. [S05] A dramatic reputation-for-endurance sentence cannot replace evidence about what market participants believed.

## 4. What the large numbers actually mean

### 4.1 The bankruptcy headline

The familiar approximately **$639 billion of assets and $613 billion of liabilities** refers to the consolidated figures cited in the filing narrative, with a **May 31, 2008** measurement date. The debtors' 2011 disclosure statement explicitly traces those amounts to Exhibit A of LBHI's voluntary petition. The relevant passage was found in a targeted search excerpt; the full disclosure statement was not read. Yale's overview corroborates the rounded filing amounts. [S19, S21]

These are not $639 billion in cash, $639 billion of investment losses, the sale value of the estate, or a fresh independent valuation taken on the morning of September 15. Consolidated figures are also not interchangeable with a parent-only asset schedule. Do not subtract two rounded filing headline numbers and announce that Lehman was definitely economically solvent: valuation uncertainty, liquidity and the ability to realize assets matter. [S05, S21]

### 4.2 Fiscal 2007 gross leverage

Lehman's fiscal year ended **November 30**, not December 31. Its 2007 10-K reports:

| Measure | Value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Total assets | $691.063 billion | Reported consolidated accounting assets at fiscal year-end |
| Total stockholders' equity | $22.490 billion | The gross-leverage denominator |
| Gross leverage | 30.7 times | Total assets divided by stockholders' equity |
| Net assets | $372.959 billion | Adjusted measure, not the same numerator as total assets |
| Tangible equity capital | $23.103 billion | Adjusted denominator, not identical to stockholders' equity |
| Reported net leverage | 16.1 times | Net assets divided by tangible equity capital |

The selected-data table was reviewed through targeted SEC search extraction ([1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/806085/000110465908005476/a08-3530_110k.htm)); definitions are also set out in examiner footnote 2854 and the following continuation. [S02, S03]

Recalculation: 691.063 / 22.490 ≈ **30.73** and 372.959 / 23.103 ≈ **16.14**, consistent with one-decimal reporting. These are our arithmetic checks, not additional source claims.

**Do not compare 30.7 directly with 12.1 and call the difference the amount hidden by Repo 105.** They are different metrics and dates. Do not call total-assets/equity 'dollars of debt per dollar of equity'; assets include the equity-funded portion.

At this leverage, a simplified example illustrates sensitivity: a loss of about 1/30.7, or 3.26% of assets, equals the starting book equity if everything else is held fixed. That is a mathematical illustration, **not** an estimate of Lehman's actual capital hole, an accounting forecast, or a claim that all assets moved together.

### 4.3 Real estate, liquidity and solvency

Yale case C reports $111 billion in commercial/residential real-estate-related assets and securities at fiscal 2007 year-end, versus $52 billion in 2006. FCIC's later $54 billion real-estate figure uses a different date and scope, excluding real estate held for sale. The documentary should not portray these as a clean like-for-like reduction without reconciliation. [S04, S05]

The 10-K's liquidity risk section acknowledges continuous dependence on secured financing, limits on moving money out of subsidiaries, and the possibility that complex assets cannot readily be sold in stressed markets. This is evidence that the business model disclosed important risks, not proof that Repo 105's use was adequately disclosed. [S02, reviewed liquidity section]

Separate:

- **Book equity:** what reported balance-sheet values imply.
- **Economic solvency:** what assets can really realize relative to obligations.
- **Liquidity:** the ability to meet payments when due.
- **Collateral availability:** which assets are unencumbered, acceptable to a particular lender and transferable from a particular entity.

A liquidity run can force fire sales that damage solvency; valuation doubts can induce a run. FCIC records disagreement about Lehman's solvency rather than settling it by repeating one manager's number. [S05]

## 5. Repo 105/108: transaction-level reconstruction

### 5.1 An ordinary repo

The cash borrower transfers securities and agrees to repurchase them later for cash plus interest. In the ordinary financing treatment, the securities remain on the borrower's books and the cash repayment obligation is recorded. Receiving cash is not revenue merely because legal documentation uses sale-and-repurchase language. [S03, S04]

Distinguish **receipt of borrowed cash** from **how the cash is subsequently used**. Receipt alone increases cash and the financing liability. If proceeds pay an existing borrowing, the mix of liabilities changes. A loose statement that a repo always leaves total assets unchanged omits the transaction baseline and use of proceeds. [S03, S04]

### 5.2 What '105' and '108' refer to

Examiner footnote 2847 distinguishes:

- Repo 105: fixed-income securities worth at least **$105 for $100 of cash**.
- Repo 108: equities worth at least **$108 for $100 of cash**.

The examiner normally uses 'Repo 105' as shorthand for **both**. This convention is explicitly stated in the report. [S03]

**Thin-detail warning:** 105% collateral-to-cash is 5% excess collateral measured against cash. If a haircut is defined as the cash discount relative to the collateral's market value, the arithmetic is 5/105 ≈ 4.76%. Similarly, 8/108 ≈ 7.41%. Many accounts loosely call these 5%/8% haircuts. Specify the denominator when producing a technical graphic rather than pretending the percentages are identical under every convention. This is our explanatory calculation from the reported collateral/cash ratios.

### 5.3 Legal isolation and effective control are different tests

SFAS 140 paragraph 9, reproduced in Yale case C's appendix, discusses isolation of transferred assets, the transferee's ability to pledge/exchange them, and the transferor's lack of effective control. Lehman's program used an English-law true-sale opinion and an interpretation of the accounting standard's control provisions. Those are related but not identical questions. [S04, appendix; S03]

The original draft reverses the law-firm fact. The examiner states that Lehman could not find the needed U.S.-law opinion and operated under **an opinion Linklaters supplied for LBIE under English law**. U.S.-based affiliates transferred inventory to LBIE for the transaction. [S03, executive summary and footnotes 2873–2875]

This does **not** establish that British courts approved misleading consolidated U.S. disclosure, or that an opinion about a transaction's English-law treatment immunized executives or auditors from every accounting/disclosure claim. Do not describe an opinion letter as a court judgment. The complete letter was not independently read in this pass.

The parsed Yale case contains confusing/inconsistent wording in its discussion of effective control. Its appendix's reproduced standard and the examiner's directly inspected executive summary are safer than copying a sentence whose negatives appear inverted. Do not reproduce that apparent textual error in a diagram. [S04, section 4 versus appendix]

### 5.4 The two steps that matter

1. **Transaction/accounting step:** Lehman recharacterizes the transaction as a sale, removes the securities inventory, receives cash and does not record the transaction as an ordinary repo borrowing. Exact entries include forward/derivative considerations; a complete journal-entry diagram must not discard them.
2. **Use-of-proceeds step:** Lehman uses the cash to repay other liabilities, reducing the reported assets and liabilities used in its leverage presentation.
3. **After the reporting date:** it borrows to repay cash plus interest, repurchases securities and brings them back onto the balance sheet.

This sequence is described directly in examiner footnotes 2852–2853 and the accompanying text. The report says inventory was generally removed for seven to ten days. [S03]

The original sentence saying a sale instantly removes the asset and a matching existing liability skips the second step. The original 'cash came back' is directionally confusing: on unwind Lehman must **pay cash out** to get its securities back, usually financed with new borrowing. 'Only the disclosure changed' is also too strong: collateral, funding costs, liquidity usage and settlement obligations were real. The misleading part concerned presentation and omission of the practice, not imaginary cash movements. [S03, S04]

### 5.5 It did not necessarily move the toxic assets

The examiner says Lehman was unable to find willing counterparties for less-liquid inventory it tried to bring into the program. It could reduce its reported overall leverage using other acceptable securities while continuing to hold difficult real-estate positions. [S03, discussion around footnotes 2867–2869]

Do not animate bad mortgages literally disappearing into a London box unless the particular assets are established. A safer visualization shows **marketable securities and cash moving**, other liabilities being repaid, and the unresolved illiquid portfolio remaining beside the shrinking reported ratio.

### 5.6 Exact volume and net-leverage comparison

| Fiscal period | Combined program amount | Reported net leverage | Counterfactual net leverage without program |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Q4 2007 | about $38.6bn | 16.1x | 17.8x |
| Q1 2008 | about $49.1bn | 15.4x | 17.3x |
| Q2 2008 | about $50.4bn | 12.1x | 13.9x |

Amounts and the convention including Repo 108 are established directly in [S03]. Net-leverage comparisons come from Yale Figure 7, which attributes them to examiner p. 748; that later printed page was **not independently reached** by the truncated primary PDF extraction. [S04]

Yale Figure 5 gives Q2 fixed-income Repo 105 of $44.536bn and equities Repo 108 of $5.847bn, totaling $50.383bn. The program figures are temporary balance-sheet amounts, not annual revenue, losses, stolen money or accumulated amounts that should be summed across dates. [S04]

The May 2008 reporting date was May 31, a Saturday; the examiner's underlying balance snapshot referenced in footnote 2880 is May 30. A graphic can use 'fiscal Q2 2008' without implying contradictory fiscal calendars. [S03]

The program began around 2001 and accelerated later. It was not invented during the final September weekend. The examiner records breached internal limits and concerns by employees; it also explicitly cautions that its discussion of whether peers still used the practice reflects employee beliefs it had not independently verified. Never turn that into 'Lehman was conclusively the only bank doing it.' [S03]

## 6. What the examiner established—and what he did not

The report is dated **March 11, 2010**. Volume 3 concerns Repo 105; the report is nine volumes, not a short regulator's press release. [S03]

The inspected executive summary says the evidence could support a fact-finder finding materially misleading reporting. It distinguishes the **failure to disclose** from whether the transactions technically complied with SFAS 140, a question it does not resolve there. [S03, footnote 2854 continuation and ending of inspected summary]

Separate four categories in narration:

1. Transaction mechanics reconstructed by the examiner.
2. Employee comments and concerns reported by the examiner.
3. The examiner's assessment of potentially actionable claims.
4. A court's eventual finding, settlement or criminal conviction.

The phrase 'accounting gimmick' appears in the report in descriptions of former employees' characterization. The draft should not manufacture a direct first-person Valukas quotation or present every employee quote as the examiner's legal conclusion. [S03, footnote 2884 and associated text]

A possible claim is not a conviction. Conversely, saying 'no criminal charges' does not establish 'not fraud.' These are different propositions, standards, defendants and proceedings.

There was civil litigation. The New York Attorney General's April 15, 2015 release describes a **$10 million settlement with Ernst & Young** arising from alleged fraud involving the transactions. It separately mentions approximately $99 million in a private federal class-action settlement. Those sums belong to different matters and should not be collapsed into one fine or verdict. [S16]

The release is an enforcement authority's characterization of allegations and the settlement, not a trial judgment against every Lehman officer. Reuters' contemporaneous search excerpt reports EY's statement that there were no findings of wrongdoing. Direct Reuters retrieval failed. This supplies the existence of the company's position, **not** an independent exoneration. [S17]

**Recommended script treatment:** attribute the examiner's misleading-disclosure conclusion, explain the legal opinion accurately, and avoid a sweeping legal verdict. An exhaustive history of all criminal investigations and civil cases is outside the evidence assembled here. If that history becomes a central claim, obtain the specific case documents and terminal orders before writing it.

Particularly relevant chronology:

| June 9, 2008 | Lehman announces preliminary Q2 loss of about $2.8bn | Preliminary announcement date is not the later finalized release. [S05] |

The September 10 company release reported improved leverage and an estimated liquidity pool while announcing losses and proposed restructuring. It is a primary record of what management publicly claimed, **not a guarantee** that each figure represented immediately accessible funding under run conditions. [S22]

## 8. Why the Barclays rescue did not close before bankruptcy

The original draft compresses multiple obstacles into 'London regulators would not approve without a government guarantee.' A more accurate account separates:

- the quality/financing of Lehman's difficult assets;
- an acquirer willing and able to buy the continuing business;
- the guarantee of trading obligations **between signing and closing**;
- Barclays shareholder approval and possible waiver;
- U.S./UK official willingness, authority and taxpayer exposure. [S05, S06]

Baxter testified that two prospective acquirers remained on Friday: Bank of America and Barclays. Bank of America instead pursued Merrill Lynch. The private-bank consortium's asset-financing effort was not the same thing as supplying the missing trading-obligations guarantee. Barclays could not deliver the needed guarantee without shareholder approval; UK authorities did not provide the waiver needed to proceed on the crisis timetable. [S06]

There is a genuine difference of perspective. The FCIC describes legal, practical, political and moral-hazard considerations, reproduces disagreement about collateral and records differing explanations over time. Baxter stresses the absence of authority to issue an unsecured unlimited guarantee and the lack of an executable buyer arrangement. These positions should be **attributed**, not blended into the narrator's declaration that rescue was either unquestionably impossible or unquestionably easy. [S05, S06]

The proposal's illiquid-asset amounts vary across accounts: Baxter describes approximately $30bn of consortium financing; the FCIC describes the contemplated asset carve-out in a roughly $40–50bn range. These may describe different proposal dimensions/stages; this pass does not reconcile them with all underlying deal documents. The revised narration uses no precise consortium number. [S05, S06]

FCIC places the filing at **1:45 a.m. Monday, September 15**. The original 'walked into a Manhattan courthouse' adds physical stage-setting that was not established. Replace it with the filing itself. The parent filed; the U.S. broker-dealer initially remained outside the parent's Chapter 11. [S05, S20]

**Important nuance:** officials did not simply provide 'no aid of any kind to any Lehman entity.' Baxter describes Fed financing of the U.S. broker-dealer after the parent's filing to support wind-down and the subsequent Barclays transfer. That is different from rescuing LBHI shareholders or guaranteeing the parent as a going concern. [S06]

## 9. Contagion: exact dates, amounts and later corrections

### Reserve Primary Fund

The September 16 fund notice says it marked **$785 million face value of Lehman debt to zero**, resulting in a stated NAV of **$0.97 per share effective at 4 p.m. New York time**. It also announced restrictions/delay on redemption proceeds. [S07]

Face value is not necessarily fair value before the announcement. Writing the holdings to zero at that moment is not proof the debt would have zero ultimate recovery. This is another reason not to turn a crisis mark or CDS auction price into a final bankruptcy recovery rate.

A subsequent **November 26 correction** says NAV should have been **$0.99 from 11 a.m. until 4 p.m. on September 16** because a spreadsheet omitted the previous day's Lehman write-down; the $0.97 figure from 4 p.m. remained. [S08]

Therefore:

- 'The fund announced 97 cents at 4 p.m.' is supported.
- 'It first became worth less than a dollar at exactly 4 p.m.' ignores the later correction.
- 'It held only Lehman commercial paper' is too narrow: contemporary evidence also discusses medium-term notes; use 'Lehman debt' unless explaining the instrument split.
- 'The whole money-fund run began only after that announcement' is too neat. FCIC and SEC material describe redemptions/funding pressure already developing. [S05, S07, S08]

### AIG

The Federal Reserve's announcement is dated **September 16, 2008, 9 p.m. EDT**. It authorizes the New York Fed to lend **up to $85bn**, with Treasury support, not a completed $85bn cash transfer the previous day. The release specifies a 24-month facility, three-month LIBOR plus 850 basis points, collateral and an expected 79.9% government equity interest. [S09]

For a nine-minute documentary, retain date, authorization, 'up to,' and the responsible institution; keep pricing/equity details in the research notes unless needed. Do not describe AIG as solely a downstream effect of Lehman's failure: FCIC records pre-existing AIG concern during the weekend. [S05, S09]

### Treasury's money-fund guarantee

Treasury's September 29 release explicitly separates the **September 19 announcement** from the **September 29 opening**. The program was for eligible funds that applied and paid a fee, covering specified holdings as of the September 19 close. Funds with NAV below $0.995 at that date were ineligible. [S10]

Do not imply that Treasury immediately restored every Reserve Primary investor to a dollar, or guaranteed every money-fund purchase from then on. The program's date, eligibility and coverage limits matter. The revised narration describes a temporary program for participating funds, not a retroactive universal guarantee.

## 10. What Barclays and Nomura actually acquired

The September asset-sale 8-K distinguishes:

- **September 16:** Barclays asset purchase agreement.
- **September 20:** bankruptcy-court approval.
- **September 22:** completion of specified U.S./Canadian investment-banking and capital-markets business and assets, with certain assumed liabilities, headquarters and data centers.
- **September 22:** Nomura announcement concerning Asia-Pacific business assets/franchises.
- **September 23:** Nomura announcement concerning European/Middle Eastern equities and investment-banking operations. [S11]

It states that the Nomura transactions did **not** include Lehman's trading assets or trading liabilities. 'Nomura took Europe and Asia' obscures the business scope and leaves listeners thinking all debts and positions traveled with the franchise. [S11]

Do not use an announcement date as a completion date. The same 8-K also describes the conditional nature of the European/Middle Eastern agreement and limited access by the U.S. parent to confidential transaction documentation. [S11]

For price graphics, contemporaneous headlines are not interchangeable with later completion terms. The inspected filing reports $250m cash plus assumed liabilities for specified Barclays assets and $1.29bn for headquarters/data centers, subject to holdbacks/prorations. This is not a clean valuation of the entire Lehman group. The revised narration omits transaction prices to avoid a digression. [S11]

## 11. The end was several different endings

### Parent plan: 2012

The March 2012 8-K records **December 6, 2011 plan confirmation** and **March 6, 2012 effectiveness**. It describes the plan trust, cancellation of existing shares, and anticipated creditor distributions. It does not describe revival of the old investment bank. [S12]

Thus 'emerged from bankruptcy in 2012' is incomplete but not necessarily false in conventional legal reporting. Safer: 'Its Chapter Eleven plan became effective in March two thousand and twelve, beginning the distribution phase of a long wind-down.' Do not equate effectiveness with the last claim, last lawsuit or final distribution.

### U.S. broker-dealer: 2022

SIPC's September 28, 2022 release reports closure of LBI's separate SIPA proceeding after approximately fourteen years. It reports:

- $106bn returned, fully satisfying 111,000 customer claims;
- 100% distributions to secured, priority and administrative creditors with allowed claims;
- more than $9.372bn distributed to unsecured general creditors, a **41.2841%** recovery;
- possible further distributions for certain unsecured general creditors from a liquidating trust. [S14]

The customer return is not a return of 100% to shareholders or all creditors. The unsecured percentage is a historical LBI class-specific result, not an eternal final rate for 'Lehman creditors.' Even a court-proceeding closure can coexist with a trust's further distributions. [S14]

### European broker-dealer: 2025

LBIE administrators' report is dated **October 14, 2025** and covers progress through October 8. It says administration ended and LBIE entered members' voluntary liquidation on **October 8, 2025**, having paid creditors and statutory interest in full. Its table describes the house-estate unsecured outcome as **100p in the pound plus statutory interest**, alongside other stakeholder categories. [S15]

This does not mean every Lehman investor worldwide made a profit or that the 2008 crisis had no economic cost. It is an entity-specific distribution outcome after a long administration. State the cutoff and distinguish house estate, client property, principal, interest and preferred equity.

### Why a single forty-cent recovery is unusable

New York Fed researchers' January 14, 2019 analysis distinguishes third-party claims from interaffiliate claims. It reports almost $94bn to third parties across sixteen distributions, estimates $304–312bn of allowed third-party claims and derives approximately **31% nominal recovery**. Discounting for delay reduces their estimate to roughly **21–26%**. It also describes different outcomes across affiliates and creditor groups. [S13]

These measures are not contradictions of LBI's 2022 41.2841% or LBIE's full principal/interest recovery; they concern different scopes, dates and denominators. Add intercompany distributions to third-party receipts indiscriminately and the same economic money can be counted more than once. Treating face-value recovery years later as equivalent to immediate payment also ignores time value and lost liquidity. [S13–S15]

**Revised-script choice:** use the cleanly bounded LBI customer versus unsecured-creditor contrast, with a September 2022 cutoff, and explain that the parent/European proceedings are different. Drop the original universal 'ultimately forty cents' sentence.

## 12. Causal conclusions and adversarial checks

### Supported synthesis

- Lehman's reported scale and small equity base left it sensitive to losses and funding stress. [S02, S05]
- Illiquid/concentrated exposures and short-term funding made renewed borrowing essential. [S02, S04, S05]
- The examiner found that undisclosed temporary quarter-end transactions materially changed its financial presentation. [S03]
- Rescue negotiations failed; the parent filed; contagion and official interventions followed in an already stressed system. [S05–S10]

### Not established

- That Repo 105 alone caused Lehman's failure or the global crisis.
- That counterparties learned the program's full details during September 12–14 and fled for that reason.
- That every reported improvement in leverage was fictitious.
- That the entire $50bn represented bad mortgages, losses, stolen cash or permanently erased debt.
- That the Fed's ability/inability to rescue was an uncontested legal fact fully resolved by a participant's statement.
- That lack of a criminal conviction proves disclosures were fair, or that an examiner's possible civil claims establish criminal guilt.
- That every creditor recovered the same proportion or that all estates ended in one year.

The documentary should hold two ideas together: **the accounting presentation mattered**, and **the underlying asset/liquidity crisis cannot be reduced to accounting presentation**. The final lesson should invite viewers to ask what a reported leverage improvement measures, whether it is temporary and how the institution funds itself—not assert an unsupported secret-discovery timeline.

## 15. Remaining work before release

The source-backed revision removes or narrows unsupported assertions rather than pretending research can prove every dramatic line. Human editorial review still needs to check the complete script, choose the thesis/title, verify that visuals do not misrepresent actual events, and approve the final cut. If the video adds detailed legal culpability, current estate totals, precise rescue valuations, transaction-specific journals or an all-time bankruptcy-ranking claim, obtain the corresponding additional primary records first.

**Bottom line:** a materially stronger and auditable documentary is possible without claiming omniscience. Precision means identifying what a fact describes—and what it does not describe.
