## Scene 1 — The Noonday Pause

The middle of the day arrives with a heavy step. Perhaps you are sitting at a desk where the papers are piled high, or standing by a workbench with hours of labor still stretching before you, and the initial energy of the morning has worn thin. Psalm 55 tells us of the psalmist's resolve to pray at evening, and morning, and at noon, crying aloud to the One who hears. Midday is not simply a break in the schedule; it is an appointed hour to lift our eyes from the immediate pressure of the task and look toward the source of our endurance. We come to this quiet moment because the afternoon requires more than human willpower. We come to stand before God in the middle of our work.

## Scene 2 — The Inheritance of Caleb

In the fourteenth chapter of the book of Joshua, Caleb stands before his leader to claim what God had promised him forty-five years earlier. He is eighty-five years old, yet his spirit is unbent by the long decades in the wilderness. He looks back upon years of waiting and forward upon a rugged, unconquered hill country still inhabited by the Anakims. He does not ask for an easy path or a level plain where the work is light. He looks directly at the obstacle and remembers the faithfulness of the Lord. As Caleb testified of his enduring vitality, we read these words from the fourteenth chapter of Joshua, verses eleven and twelve: As yet I am as strong this day as I was in the day that Moses sent me: as my strength was then, even so is my strength now, for war, both to go out, and to come in. Now therefore give me this mountain, whereof the LORD spake in that day; for thou heardest in that day how the Anakims were there, and that the cities were great and fenced: if so be the LORD will be with me, then I shall be able to drive them out, as the LORD said.

## Scene 3 — Facing the Steep Ascent

Caleb did not look at his own age as a disqualification, nor did he look at the great and fenced cities as a permanent barrier. He remembered that the promise of God outlasts the difficulty of the terrain. When we face our own mountains in the middle of the day — whether it is a difficult decision at work, a strained relationship, or simply the sheer weight of responsibility that will not lift — we are tempted to ask for smaller tasks and easier days. But God often reserves His greatest demonstrations of sustaining grace for the steep ascents. Matthew Henry notes in his commentary that true faith does not shrink from giant obstacles; it measures the difficulty only to magnify the power of the One who helps us. Let us pray now for the courage to face the afternoon without shrinking back. Lord, when the tasks before us look formidable and our own strength feels small, grant us the steady confidence that comes from resting in Your promises. Remind us that You are not distant from our daily labor, but present in the very center of our responsibilities.

## Scene 4 — The Sustaining Strength

The strength that Caleb claimed was not his own natural vigor renewed by time; it was the divine strength that sustained him through every year of wilderness wandering. In our own work, the afternoon hours often expose our limitations. We discover how quickly our patience frays and how easily our focus wanders. Yet the promise remains that God gives power to the faint and increases strength to those who have no might. Let us bring our weariness to Him right now, asking not for an escape from our duties, but for the grace to perform them with integrity and peace. Father, renew our minds as the day presses on. Where our patience is wearing thin, grant us Your patience. Where our hands are tired, steady them with Your power. Keep us from the temptation to cut corners or rush through our obligations with bitterness. Let our work this afternoon be offered as unto You, quiet and steadfast.

## Scene 5 — Standing Firm in the Work

To ask for the mountain is to ask for the place of testing and the place of victory combined. It is a prayer that refuses to settle for complacency. As we return to the remaining hours of our day, we ask that our thoughts and our actions be established by Your hand. May every conversation, every decision, and every quiet task be marked by the calm assurance of Your presence. Lord God of heaven, let Your grace rest upon our labor. Guard our hearts from anxiety about what remains unfinished. Give us wisdom to address each problem as it arises, and give us a quiet spirit that trusts in Your timing and Your provision. When the noise of the day presses in around us, let Your peace keep our minds stayed upon You.

## Scene 6 — The Midday Blessing

As the sun stands at its height and the afternoon stretches out before us, we rest our hands in the care of our Creator. We leave our burdens here at the throne of grace, knowing that He who led us through the morning will sustain us through the evening shadows. May the Lord bless you and keep you.
