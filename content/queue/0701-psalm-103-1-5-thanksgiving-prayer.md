## Scene 1 — Speaking to Your Own Soul

Grace and peace to you, and welcome to The Praise Garden. In this prayer we follow David as he does something unusual: he talks to himself. Hear the King James Scripture in Psalm one hundred three, verses one and two: Bless the LORD, O my soul: and all that is within me, bless his holy name. Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits.

David does not wait until he feels like praising. He calls his own soul to attention. He knows how quickly the heart forgets, how a single worry can crowd out a hundred kindnesses. So he gives himself an instruction: forget not. Lord, we confess that we forget easily. We remember the slight and lose the mercy. Gather our scattered thoughts now. Let everything within us, our memory, our affections, and our words, turn toward You and bless Your holy name.

## Scene 2 — Thankful for Forgiveness First

When David begins his list of benefits, he does not start with comfort or success. Hear Psalm one hundred three, verse three: Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases.

Forgiveness comes first. Later in the same psalm, in verses ten through twelve, David explains how far that forgiveness reaches: He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities. For as the heaven is high above the earth, so great is his mercy toward them that fear him. As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.

Father, thank You that You have not treated us as our sins deserve. Thank You for every wrong pardoned and every burden of guilt lifted through Jesus Christ. And we thank You that You care for our bodies as well as our souls. For those who are sick today, we ask Your mercy and Your help, and we thank You for doctors, nurses, and medicine as gifts of Your kindness.

## Scene 3 — Redeemed and Crowned

David's list continues. Hear Psalm one hundred three, verse four: Who redeemeth thy life from destruction; who crowneth thee with lovingkindness and tender mercies.

Two pictures stand side by side. The first is rescue: a life pulled back from ruin. Many of us can look behind us and see a place where things could have ended very differently, and did not. The second is a crown. God does not merely pull us out and leave us at the edge. He sets lovingkindness and tender mercies upon us like a crown upon a head, as though we were honoured guests.

Lord, thank You for the times You kept us when we did not know we were in danger. Thank You for the turning point, the closed door, the warning we heeded, the hand that reached us in time. And thank You that Your mercy toward us is not reluctant. You crown us with it. Let us carry ourselves today as people who are loved.

## Scene 4 — Satisfied With Good Things

Hear Psalm one hundred three, verse five: Who satisfieth thy mouth with good things; so that thy youth is renewed like the eagle's.

Here is thanksgiving for ordinary provision and for renewed strength. A meal, a night of rest, a morning when the tiredness has lifted: these are easy to overlook because they come so often. James chapter one, verse seventeen reminds us where they come from: Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.

Father of lights, thank You for every good thing that has reached us from Your hand. Thank You for bread, for laughter, for work, for rest, and for the people who share them with us. Where strength has run low, renew it. We do not ask in order to demand, and we do not measure Your love by how much we have. We simply thank You that You are a giver.

## Scene 5 — When Remembering Is Hard

There are seasons when a list of benefits is difficult to write. David knew that too. Hear Psalm one hundred three, verses thirteen and fourteen: Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him. For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.

God is not surprised by our weakness. He remembers what we are made of. And in the book of Lamentations, written among ruins, we find these words in chapter three, verses twenty-two and twenty-three: It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

Compassionate Father, be gentle with every listener who is grieving, exhausted, or numb. They do not need to feel grateful to be held by You. Let one small mercy be visible to them today, and let that be enough. Give them people to lean on, and the willingness to ask for help.

## Scene 6 — Bless the LORD, O My Soul

The psalm ends where it began, but wider. Hear Psalm one hundred three, verse twenty-two: Bless the LORD, all his works in all places of his dominion: bless the LORD, O my soul. David has called the whole creation to praise, and then he returns to his own heart, because that is the one voice he can offer himself.

So here is a simple practice. Take David's four phrases, forgiven, healed, redeemed, crowned, and beside each one, name a moment from your own life. Then say it aloud: Bless the LORD, O my soul.

Heavenly Father, we will not forget Your benefits. Thank You for pardon, for rescue, for daily bread, and for mercies that are new every morning. Keep thanksgiving on our lips in every season. In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord. Type Amen to agree with this prayer, and subscribe to The Praise Garden to enter His gates with daily thanksgiving and praise.
