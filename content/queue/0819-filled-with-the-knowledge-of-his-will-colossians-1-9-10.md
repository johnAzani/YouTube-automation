## Scene 1 — Bringing the Decision to the Lord

There are times when a person stands before a crossroad in life, holding two different paths in their hands, wondering which one leads further into peace and which one leads into confusion. You may be sitting in a quiet room tonight or facing the early hours of a new day, carrying a question that has no simple answer. You have weighed the facts, you have listened to the opinions of others, and still the right direction remains veiled behind a quiet mist. It is easy in such moments to lean upon our own understanding, to run ahead of the cloud, and to try to force a door to open that God has not yet touched. But wisdom begins the moment we lay our own calculations down and turn our faces toward the One who sees the entire road from its beginning to its end. The apostle Paul, writing to the believers at Colosse, understood that human effort alone cannot pierce the fog of uncertainty. He kneeled before the Father with an earnest, unceasing intercession for those who were seeking to walk faithfully. Hear the exact words of Colossians chapter 1, verses 9 and 10, as they are recorded for us in the King James Version:

For this cause we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you, and to desire that ye might be filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding; That ye might walk worthy of the Lord unto all pleasing, being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God.

Heavenly Father, we come before Thee tonight acknowledging that our own understanding is short and our sight is dim. We do not ask today for a shortcut around the valley, nor do we demand that Thou wouldst lay out every mile of the years ahead before our eyes. We ask simply for the quiet assurance of Thy presence as we stand at this junction. When our thoughts are scattered and our judgment is clouded by our own desires or fears, still our hearts. Teach us to wait upon Thee, and grant us the grace to seek Thy kingdom first, knowing that all other things needful shall be added in Thy perfect time.

## Scene 2 — Asking God for Wisdom

The request that Paul makes for the Colossians is not for a sudden flash of worldly ambition or a louder voice to argue our case in the world. He prays that they might be filled with the knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding. Matthew Henry, in his commentary on these verses, reminds us that true knowledge must govern the affections and regulate the life, rising far above mere speculation into a divine light that illuminates the daily walk. When we lack wisdom, James tells us to ask of God, who gives to all men liberally and upbraideth not. Yet asking requires a quiet posture. It asks us to empty our hands of our own predetermined outcomes so that God might fill them with His counsel. We often come to prayer with our minds already made up, asking God to bless the path we have already chosen in our hearts. True discernment begins when we tell the Lord that we prefer His closed door to our own open one, and His delayed answer to our rushed certainty.

Lord God, search our hearts today and remove every hidden desire that runs contrary to Thy holy will. We confess that we are often impatient, wanting the answer today so that we can be free of the weight of waiting. Give us the humility to ask for wisdom without demanding to dictate its shape. When the path divides and we cannot discern the right turn from the left, grant us that spiritual understanding which comes not from human cleverness, but from communion with Thy Spirit. Keep us from leaning upon our own wisdom, and let Thy truth be the lamp that guides our feet.

## Scene 3 — The Lamp of the Word

The Scriptures remind us that God's word is a lamp unto our feet and a light unto our path. Notice the modesty of that metaphor: a lamp does not illuminate the entire horizon; it sheds just enough light for the next single step in the dark. We often grow anxious because we are trying to see five years down a road that God has left hidden in grace. We want a blueprint when the Lord offers a relationship. We want a guarantee when the Lord asks for trust. The prayer of Colossians points us back to the steady cultivation of spiritual understanding, rooted day by day in the unchanging truth of Scripture. As we read, meditate, and steep our minds in the voice of God through His word, the noise of our own anxieties begins to recede, and the gentle prompting of the Spirit becomes clear.

Father, we thank Thee that Thy word does not leave us without a light, even when the night around us is deep. Forgive us for the times we have looked for signs in the sky while ignoring the quiet instruction of Thy Holy Scriptures. Teach us to treasure Thy commandments and to hide Thy word in our hearts so that we might not turn aside to the right hand or to the left. When we feel pressured to make a hasty choice, quiet our spirit and bring to our remembrance the truths we have learned from Thy mouth. Let Thy word steady our steps and quiet our fears.

## Scene 4 — Wise Counsel and Tested Motives

Walking worthy of the Lord unto all pleasing also means looking honestly at our own motives and welcoming the sober counsel of those who walk closely with God. Proverbs tells us that in the multitude of counsellors there is safety, and that the way of a fool is right in his own eyes. When we face a weighty decision, our own heart can easily deceive us, dressing our personal preferences in the language of spiritual duty. We need the faithful wounds of a friend, the grounded wisdom of mature believers who can help us test whether our desires align with the scriptures. At the same time, we remember that human counsel is always secondary to the voice of the Shepherd. We bring every piece of advice, every open door, and every closed gate back to the throne of grace, asking the Lord to try our reins and test our thoughts.

Lord, bring godly counsel across our path when we are prone to wander or prone to self-deception. Give us teachable spirits that are willing to listen when others speak wisdom from Thy word. Guard us against pride that refuses correction and stubbornness that insists on its own way. If our motives are mixed with fear, ambition, or impatience, purify them by Thy grace. We place our relationships, our work, and our future choices into Thy hands, asking Thee to make our path straight according to Thy righteousness.

## Scene 5 — Waiting With Patience and Ordered Steps

The second verse of our passage reminds us that this filling with wisdom has a purpose: that ye might walk worthy of the Lord unto all pleasing, being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God. A life that pleases God is not measured by how fast it moves, but by how faithfully it abides. Waiting upon the Lord is often the hardest part of discernment. We live in a world that praises speed and demands immediate resolution, but God often works in seasons of quiet cultivation, strengthening our roots before He brings fruit to the branch. If you are in a season where the answer has not yet come, take courage. The steps of a good man are ordered by the Lord, and He delighteth in his way. Even if he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down, for the Lord upholdeth him with His hand.

Father, we pray for the grace of holy patience. When the silence of heaven stretches longer than our comfort desires, keep us from taking matters into our own hands out of sheer exhaustion. Help us to walk worthy of the vocation wherewith we are called, showing kindness, steadfastness, and faith in the ordinary tasks of today. Let our lives bear the quiet fruit of the Spirit — love, joy, peace, gentleness, and goodness — even while we wait for the clearer direction of tomorrow. Sustain those whose strength is failing, and remind them that Thou art holding them fast.

## Scene 6 — Committing the Way and Closing Benediction

We return now to the quiet assurance with which we began. The God who inspired the apostle to pray this prayer for the saints in Colosse is the same God who listens to our quiet petitions tonight. He does not mock our hesitation, nor is He impatient with our human weakness. He invites us to cast our care upon Him because He careth for us. As we close this time of prayer, we do not ask for a guarantee of ease, nor do we demand to know the end from the beginning. We ask simply for the presence of the Guide Himself, trusting that where He leads, His grace will be sufficient for every mile.

May the Lord bless thee and keep thee. May the Lord make His face shine upon thee and be gracious unto thee. May the Lord lift up His countenance upon thee, and fill thee with the knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding, that thou mayest walk worthy of Him unto all pleasing, fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God, now and forevermore.
