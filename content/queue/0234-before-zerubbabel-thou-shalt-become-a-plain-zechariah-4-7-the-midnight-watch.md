## Scene 1 — Rising at the Midnight Hour

The quiet of the midnight hour has settled over the house, and perhaps in the silence, an obstruction has come to mind that feels entirely immovable. It may be a barrier in your work, a long-standing trial in your home, or a heavy weight upon your spirit that refuses to lift as the night deepens. Like the watchman standing upon the wall in the darkness, this is the hour appointed for prayer, not for quiet surrender to despair, but for rising to give thanks unto God because of His righteous judgments, echoing the posture of the Psalmist who awoke at midnight to seek the face of the Living God.

When the prophet Zechariah received the word of the Lord, Zerubbabel and the remnant in Jerusalem were facing a massive undertaking. The temple lay in ruins, surrounded by adversaries and seemingly impossible discouragements that towered over them like an insurmountable peak. Yet the word of the Lord came not by might, nor by power, but by His Spirit. In this midnight hour, we bring every towering obstacle before the throne of grace, acknowledging that what looks like a permanent wall to human eyes is entirely subject to the word of the Almighty.

O Lord, our God, creator of the ends of the earth, we come before Thee in this still and solemn watch of the night. Thou knowest the weights that press upon the minds of Thy people, and Thou seest the barriers that block the path of obedience and faith. We do not lean upon our own strength, for we are frail and easily overwhelmed by the magnitude of our trials. We look unto Thee, O Lord, whose throne is established in the heavens and whose dominion rules over all. In this midnight hour, we quiet our restless thoughts and fix our gaze upon Thy holy word, asking that Thou wouldst grant us the grace to stand firm upon the watchtower and wait upon Thy salvation.

## Scene 2 — Praise Inside the Midnight Trial

There are seasons when the trial does not move at our command, and the night feels exceptionally long. In the sixteenth chapter of the book of Acts, Paul and Silas found themselves in the inner prison, their feet fast in the stocks after enduring bitter stripes for the name of Christ. Yet at midnight, they did not yield to bitterness or complaints; they prayed and sang praises unto God, and the prisoners heard them. Matthew Henry observes that true faith finds a song even in the dungeon, because the darkness of the circumstance cannot extinguish the light of God's presence.

When a great mountain rises in your path, the natural inclination is to measure its height and despair of ever crossing over it. But the discipline of midnight prayer teaches us to look away from the obstacle and look unto the God who carved the mountains out of the dust. We do not manufacture artificial joy, nor do we pretend that the trial is light; rather, we bring the very heaviness of our hearts and place it under the weight of God's unchanging promises. As we sing His praises in the night, our perspective shifts from the size of the mountain to the greatness of our Redeemer.

Father, we confess that when trials surround us, our eyes are too often fixed upon the walls of our confinement rather than upon the God who opens doors. Forgive us for our unbelief and for the anxious thoughts that rob us of Thy peace during the watches of the night. Teach us the sacrifice of praise when the way is dark and uncertain. Let our voices rise in authentic worship, not because the burden is small, but because Thou art great and greatly to be praised. Strengthen our inner man by Thy Spirit, that we may not faint when the testing is prolonged, but rather prove steadfast in our reliance upon Thy grace.

## Scene 3 — Standing Upon the Watchtower

To stand upon the watchtower is to refuse the comfort of complacency and to keep awake when the world sleeps. In the prophecy of Habakkuk, the servant of God declares that he will stand upon his watch, and set him upon the tower, and will watch to see what God will say unto him. In our own lives, standing on the watch means refusing to let go of God's promises just because the dawn has not yet broken. It is an act of holy persistence, holding fast to the covenant when every earthly circumstance suggests that the battle has been lost.

Zerubbabel had been given a specific task by God: to lay the foundation of the house and to finish it. But the opposition was fierce, and the hands of the builders often grew tired. It is easy in the Christian life to grow weary of well-doing, to look at the half-built walls of our families, our ministries, or our own spiritual discipline, and wonder if the work will ever be completed. Yet God does not leave His work half-done. He who gives the mandate also supplies the grace to bring forth the headstone with shoutings of triumph.

Lord God of hosts, we place ourselves upon the watchtower tonight on behalf of our homes, our loved ones, and our own weak hearts. We bring before Thee the unfinished tasks, the broken places, and the areas of our lives where discouragement has whispered that we should give up. Hold us up by Thy right hand when our strength fails. Give us the endurance of the faithful watchman who looks through the darkest hours of the night, waiting for the first sign of Thy merciful intervention. Let no weariness cause us to abandon the place of prayer, and let our hearts remain anchored in Thy truth.

## Scene 4 — Loosening of Heavy Burdens & Spiritual Breakthrough

Now we come to the heart of the prophetic declaration given to Zerubbabel, words that pierce through every human limitation and shatter every spiritual barrier. Listen carefully to the voice of the Scripture as it is written in the fourth chapter of Zechariah, verse seven:

Who art thou, O great mountain? before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain: and he shall bring forth the headstone thereof with shoutings, crying, Grace, grace unto it.

The mountain that seemed so high, so rugged, and so permanent does not intimidate the God of heaven. He speaks to it directly: Who art thou, O great mountain? Before His appointed servant, that very mountain shall become a plain. There is no obstacle too stubborn for His word, no stronghold too fortified for His Spirit. And when the work is finished, the headstone is brought forth not with human boasting, not with the pride of our own achievements, but with the cry of grace, grace unto it from first to last.

O Sovereign Lord, before whom every mountain must bow and every rough place must be made smooth, we bring our great mountains unto Thee tonight. Thou seest the specific trials that loom over our lives — the obstacles to peace, the barriers to righteousness, the entanglements of fear and doubt. We ask Thee, by the word of Thy power, to speak to these mountains. Where there is a barrier that man cannot remove, let Thy Spirit move and make that mountain a plain. We disclaim all human merit and all self-reliance; we lay down our own weapons and cry out for Thy sovereign grace to accomplish what we never could.

## Scene 5 — Divine Turning Point & Guarded Household

Every midnight intercession is a turning point where the darkness gives way to the certainty of God's sustaining care. When we leave our burdens at the foot of His throne, we are not left to wander in uncertainty. The Apostle Paul reminds us in the thirteenth chapter of Hebrews that the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant, will make us perfect in every good work to do His will. His protection encompasses our homes, our minds, and our coming days.

To watch through the midnight hours is to place a hedge of prayer around those we love, committing their steps, their thoughts, and their futures into the hands of the faithful Creator. The same God who sustained Zerubbabel through every political intrigue and physical discouragement watches over His people today. His angels encamp round about them that fear Him. As we rest our souls upon His unchanging word, the anxieties that accompanied us into the night begin to lose their grip, replaced by a deep and settled trust in His providence.

Heavenly Father, we commit our households and our futures into Thy hands during this midnight watch. Surround our families with Thy favor as with a shield. Guard our minds from the alarms of the night and the terrors that walk in darkness. Where there is discord, bring Thy peace; where there is weakness, manifest Thy strength. We rest in the assurance that Thou art working even when we perceive it not, and that every trial permitted by Thy wisdom is designed to draw us closer to Thy heart and reveal the glory of Thy grace.

## Scene 6 — Midnight Thanksgiving & Watchman's Benediction

As this midnight watch draws to its close, our hearts turn from petition to deep, reverent thanksgiving. We rose in the darkness bearing heavy weights, but we leave this hour with our faces lifted toward the God who hears and answers prayer. The mountain that looked insurmountable has been addressed by His word, and whether the change is seen immediately or worked out in the quietness of His timing, we trust the builder and maker of all things. His grace began the work, His grace sustains it, and with shouts of grace, grace unto it, He will bring it to completion.

May the Lord bless you and keep you through the remainder of this night. May He cause His face to shine upon you and grant you His supernatural peace. May He make every mountain in your path a plain before His glory, and may your heart remain steadfast upon the watchtower of faith until the dawn breaks.
