## Scene 1 — Cause Me to Hear Thy Lovingkindness in the Morning (Psalm 143:8)

Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning; for in thee do I trust: cause me to know the way wherein I should walk; for I lift up my soul unto thee.

Before this day says a single word to you, let the first voice you hear be the voice of His lovingkindness. Before the phone fills with demands, before yesterday's worries try to name today, David teaches us to ask for one thing first: to hear mercy in the morning. Not at the end of the day, when strength is spent. Not after we have tried every other door. In the morning, while the light is still new and the path is still unwritten, God delights to speak kindness over the day He is about to give.

Heavenly Father, this morning I ask You to let me hear Your lovingkindness before I hear anything else. Let Your mercy be the first headline over my life today, louder than every fear, every deadline and every memory of failure. I trust in You, not in my own strength, not in people, not in outcomes I cannot control. Speak, Lord, and let my heart recognise Your voice. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Scene 2 — For in Thee Do I Trust (Psalm 143:8)

Trust is the hinge of this prayer. David does not say, cause me to hear because I have earned it, or because I have never failed. He says, for in thee do I trust. Spurgeon observed that the morning is the fitting season for this confidence, because the believer who begins with trust does not have to borrow peace later from circumstances. The day may bring news we did not choose, but it cannot bring a moment where God has stopped being worthy of trust.

Lord, I declare my trust in You at the start of this day. Where I am tempted to rehearse every worst outcome, teach me to rehearse Your character instead. You have carried me through nights I thought would never end, and You have never once abandoned Your own. So I will not walk into today braced for abandonment. I walk in held, kept and beloved. Settle my breathing, steady my thoughts, and let trust, not anxiety, set the pace of everything that follows. Amen.

## Scene 3 — Cause Me to Know the Way Wherein I Should Walk (Psalm 143:8, 10)

Cause me to know the way wherein I should walk; for I lift up my soul unto thee. Teach me to do thy will; for thou art my God: thy spirit is good; lead me into the land of uprightness.

Notice that David asks to know the way, not merely the destination. So much of our morning anxiety comes from demanding the whole map when God is offering the next faithful step. Matthew Henry reminds us that those who lift up their souls to God may expect to be guided by His Spirit, step by step, into ground that is even and upright. You do not need to solve the whole year before breakfast. You need the way wherein you should walk today, and the God who knows that way has already gone before you into it.

Father, show me the way I should walk today. In the decisions waiting at work, in the conversations I cannot rehearse, in the private choices nobody else will see, lead me into the land of uprightness. Close the doors that only flatter my pride, and make plain the path that carries Your peace. Where I cannot see far, give me light enough for the next step, and courage enough to take it. Thy Spirit is good; let Him lead me. Amen.

## Scene 4 — I Flee unto Thee to Hide Me (Psalm 143:9)

Deliver me, O LORD, from mine enemies: I flee unto thee to hide me.

There is no shame in this movement of the prayer. David, the warrior king, begins his morning not by boasting of his strength but by running to his hiding place. Some opposition you will face today is visible: a hard meeting, an unfair word, a pressure that has a name. Some of it is quieter: discouragement, temptation, the old accuser replaying old sins. The morning altar is where we stop pretending we are our own shelter. We flee to God first, and from that shelter we face everything else.

Lord, this morning I flee unto You to hide me. Be my refuge before You are my strategy. Hide my mind from tormenting thoughts, hide my family from every scheme of darkness, and hide my heart from bitterness when people are difficult. Deliver me from enemies I can see and from battles I cannot name. Under Your covering I am not exposed, and I am not alone. Let Your favour surround me today as a shield on every road I travel. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Scene 5 — A Blessing over the Work of This Day (Psalm 143:10)

Thy spirit is good; lead me into the land of uprightness.

Now the prayer turns outward, because a morning with God was never meant to end with us alone. The same Spirit who leads us into upright ground goes with us into ordinary places: the desk, the road, the market, the classroom, the hospital corridor, the home that needs our patience. To be led by God is not to escape the day; it is to enter it clean, honest and unafraid, carrying His presence into rooms that have not yet heard a kind word.

Holy Spirit, establish the work of my hands today. Bless the labour I understand and the tasks I would rather avoid. Bless my family as they go out, and keep them as they come in. Let my words carry grace into every room, my decisions carry honesty in private, and my presence carry peace where there has been tension. Where doors should open, open them in Your favour. Where they should stay closed, give me contentment to trust You. Lead me, and I will follow. Amen.

## Scene 6 — I Lift Up My Soul unto Thee: The Sending (Psalm 143:8)

Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning; for in thee do I trust.

Hear that promise one more time as you step toward the door. You are not sending yourself into this day; you are being sent, by a God who has already spoken mercy over it. The lovingkindness you heard in the quiet is not left behind in the quiet. It walks with you into traffic and meetings, into waiting rooms and workplaces, into every ordinary hour where faith is actually lived. Lift up your soul to Him, and keep it lifted.

Father, I lift up my soul unto You for all of today. I receive Your lovingkindness as my portion this morning, Your guidance as my path, and Your presence as my shield. Let me return tonight with a testimony of how You led me. Until tomorrow's sunrise, keep me walking in Your way. I begin this day at Your altar, and I go in Your peace. In the mighty name of Jesus, amen. Type Amen to agree with this prayer, and subscribe to The Morning Altar to begin every sunrise with us.
