## Scene 1 — The Pause at High Noon

The clock moves past twelve, the sunlight strikes the window glass at its highest angle, and the weight of the day presses in upon the mind. This is the hour when the morning’s enthusiasm has burned away and the evening’s rest is still far off. You are sitting at a desk, standing at a workbench, or moving through the demanding noise of a shift that feels longer with every passing hour. Step away from the urgency for these few moments. Let the breath settle in your chest. The noon hour is not merely a break in the schedule; it is an appointed meeting place between your labour and the mercy of God. Psalm 55 17 tells us of this exact posture, declaring that evening and morning and at noon will we pray and cry aloud, and He shall hear our voice. We do not wait for the sun to set before we seek help. Right here, in the glare of the heavy afternoon, we turn our faces toward the source of life. Let the noise of demands fade for a moment. Let the tension in your shoulders soften. The prophet Jeremiah left us an image of resilience that speaks directly to the dry places of our working lives. Listen to the ancient promise recorded in Jeremiah 17:7-8: Blessed is the man that trusteth in the LORD, and whose hope the LORD is. For he shall be as a tree planted by the waters, and that spreadeth out her roots by the river, and shall not see when heat cometh, but her leaf shall be green; and shall not be careful in the year of drought, neither shall cease from yielding fruit.

## Scene 2 — The Unseen Source Beneath the Surface

The metaphor of the tree planted by the waters speaks to a reality far deeper than the surface conditions of our day. Matthew Henry, in his commentary on this passage, notes that those who make God their hope do not draw their life from the changing weather above them, but from a hidden, inexhaustible source below. When the heat of the day bears down upon your work, the temptation is to look only at the dryness around you — the mounting tasks, the complicated decisions, the pressures that seem to multiply by the hour. But the tree by the river does not inspect the sun with anxiety. It does not measure the temperature of the air to determine its vitality. Its roots are already reaching downward, passing beneath the dry soil, plunging deep into the unseen moisture of the riverbank. At midday, when the world feels parched and your own strength feels thoroughly depleted, remember where your roots are anchored. Your capacity does not depend on the absence of difficulty. It depends on the presence of the living water that flows from the throne of grace. You may feel the heat of the afternoon sun beating upon your endeavors, but the inner life given by God does not wither with the temperature. Let us ask the Lord right now to draw our thoughts downward, away from the surface panic of the shift, down into the quiet depths of His unchanging faithfulness.

## Scene 3 — Standing Firm When Heat Cometh

The text does not say that the tree planted by the waters lives in a climate where heat never arrives. The heat comes for everyone. The trial, the sudden complication, the difficult conversation, the unexpected burden — these things belong to the common experience of living in a fallen world. The promise is not the absence of heat, but the absence of fear when the heat cometh. Charles Spurgeon, writing on the imagery of the Psalms and the prophets, reminds us that a believer is shielded not by the removal of trials, but by the infusion of divine grace that renders the trial harmless to the soul's deepest vitality. When the afternoon brings its testing — when the tasks are heavy and the spirit feels thin — you do not need to panic. The heat is real, but the river is deeper than the heat is hot. God has not placed you in a sheltered greenhouse where no wind blows; He has planted you by a living stream where your roots can go down deeper into His truth. Let this midday hour be a conscious deepening of those roots. Let go of the frantic effort to manufacture your own coolness. Turn away from the desperate attempt to shield yourself through your own cleverness. Rest your weight upon the Lord. Confess to Him right now that the afternoon is hot, the demands are high, and your own patience is wearing thin. He hears that honesty, and in response, He pours fresh, quiet strength into the secret places of your heart.

## Scene 4 — Unfading Leaves in the Midst of Labor

There is a remarkable phrase in the second verse of our passage: and her leaf shall be green. In the height of the dry season, when surrounding vegetation turns brittle and brown, the tree by the river retains its color. Its greenness is not a display of pride or a manufactured facade; it is the natural consequence of being connected to a supply that does not fail. In your daily work — whether you are dealing with numbers, machinery, children, clients, or difficult colleagues — your witness and your peace are meant to be like that green leaf. When those around you are panicked by the dryness of the hour, your steadiness can be a silent testimony to the goodness of God. This does not mean you will never feel tired. It means your weariness will not curdle into bitterness. It means the afternoon sun will scorch your skin, perhaps, but it will not reach the sap of your spirit. God sustains the inner person when the outer person is hard at work. He preserves your kindness when rudeness surrounds you. He preserves your integrity when shortcuts are tempting. Take a breath right now in the middle of your shift and ask the Holy Spirit to keep your leaf green. Ask Him to refresh your spirit with the quiet assurance that He is walking through these afternoon hours with you, guarding your mind from despair and your heart from resentment.

## Scene 5 — Unceasing Fruitfulness Without Striving

The final promise of the passage is equally steady: and shall not be careful in the year of drought, neither shall cease from yielding fruit. The word careful here in the old language means full of care, anxious, anxious-hearted, frantically spinning solutions out of worry. A tree does not fret over the lack of rain if its roots are already drinking from the river. It does not strain and sweat to produce fruit; the fruit comes simply because the life within is abundant and unimpeded. How much of our midday exhaustion comes from this very thing — straining to produce results by our own anxiety, worrying over the outcome of things we cannot control, carrying the whole weight of the work as if God were on vacation. The Lord invites you to lay down that frantic care during this midday pause. You do not have to force the harvest today. You only need to abide in the One who gives the increase. Let the anxiety of the unfinished project drop from your hands. Trust that the work done in dependence upon Him is never wasted, even when the results are slow to appear. Let your heart be unburdened of the frantic need to prove yourself before noon gives way to evening. The God who planted you by these waters is well able to bring forth fruit in your life, quietly, steadily, and in His own good time.

## Scene 6 — The Benediction of the Afternoon

As this midday watch draws to its close, we gather the loose threads of our concentration and place them entirely into the hands of our covenant God. You are standing in the middle of the day, but you are not standing alone. The heat of the shift may continue, the afternoon tasks may still stretch out before you, but the river of God’s grace flows just as strongly now as it did at dawn. Take His peace with you as you step back into your labor. Let His strength steady your hands and quiet your thoughts for the hours that remain.
