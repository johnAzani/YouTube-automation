## Scene 1 — Bringing the Decision to the Lord

Heavenly Father, we come before Your throne today with hearts that desire to honor You in every step we take. Lord, You are the God who sees the end from the beginning, and Your Word is a sure anchor when the path before us is shrouded in mist and uncertainty. In Psalm thirty-two, verse eight, You have given us this wonderful and comforting promise: I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go; I will guide thee with mine eye. Lord, we acknowledge that of ourselves we do not know the way. We confess our human limitations, our prone tendency to lean upon our own understanding, and our deep need for Your heavenly direction. 

As we open our hearts to You this day, we lay bare the decisions, crossroads, and questions that weigh heavily upon our minds. You know the exact nature of the choices before us — whether they touch our work, our relationships, our ministries, or the daily turns of our lives. We do not ask simply for an easy road, Father, but for a right road. We ask for a teachable spirit that is willing to be corrected, shaped, and led by Your sovereign hand. Charles Spurgeon, writing upon the rich mercy of divine guidance, reminds us that the Lord's eye is not an eye of harsh surveillance, but the tender, watchful eye of a loving parent guiding a child through a dark place. Lord, let that gentle, watchful eye rest upon us now. Quiet our anxious thoughts, still our hurried impulses, and grant us the grace to trust in Your unfailing wisdom with all our hearts. We refuse to lean on our own limited insight, and instead, we place our hands into Yours, asking You to order our steps according to Your holy Word.

## Scene 2 — Asking God for Wisdom

O Lord God of heaven and earth, James reminds us in his epistle that if any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him. Father, we confess that we do lack wisdom. We look at the complexities of our current season and we realize how easily we could miss Your best if left to our own devices. We do not come with demanding spirits, but with empty hands, asking that You would fill us with the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of You. Matthew Henry, in his commentary on the prayers of the saints, observes that God is never weary of the petitions of His children who ask for spiritual understanding rather than selfish indulgence. 

Grant us, therefore, discernment to distinguish between the loud voices of the world and the still, small voice of Your Spirit. Teach us to weigh every motive and every opportunity against the unchanging truth of Scripture. When we are tempted to rush ahead out of fear or impatience, pull us back. When we are tempted to hesitate out of timidity or doubt, give us holy courage. We ask for discernment that is pure, peaceable, gentle, and easy to be entreated, full of mercy and good fruits. Lord, purify our desires so that what we want most is what You want for us. As we seek Your face, grant us the assurance that You do not mock our confusion, nor do You turn away our sincere prayers for guidance. You give liberally, and we receive Your instruction with humble and grateful hearts.

## Scene 3 — The Lamp of the Word

Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path, declares the Psalmist in Psalm one hundred and nineteen. Lord, we thank You that You have not left us in total darkness, wandering aimlessly without a standard. Your Holy Word is the true and trustworthy light that illuminates our way. In moments of indecision, we often wish for a floodlight that would reveal every mile of the journey ahead, showing us the distant horizon and all the turns we will ever face. Yet Your grace ordinarily provides a lamp unto our feet — giving us just enough light for the very next step we must take in obedience. 

Father, help us to be faithful with the light we have today. Teach us not to stumble over anxieties about tomorrow, but to walk in the immediate obedience of Your commandments. Isaiah records Your promise: And thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left. Grant us sensitive ears, Lord, attuned to the gentle promptings of Your Holy Spirit through the Scriptures. Whenever we face a fork in the road, let Your Word speak clearly into our conscience, correcting our errors, confirming our steps, and bringing a deep, settled peace that surpasses all human understanding. We anchor our souls in Your truth, knowing that Your counsel shall stand forever.

## Scene 4 — Wise Counsel and Tested Motives

Father, You have also ordained that in the multitude of counsellors there is safety. As we seek Your guidance for the decisions before us, we ask for the humility to receive godly counsel from those mature believers You have placed around us. We recognize that while prayer is our primary resort, You often use the wise advice of others, the faithful fellowship of the saints, and sound, practical judgment to confirm the direction of Your Spirit. We also remember that prayer walks alongside the good and orderly use of professional counsel, and we give thanks for the doctors, advisors, and counselors whose expertise assists us in navigating life's practical duties. 

Lord, search our hearts and try our thoughts. If there is any hidden pride, any stubborn self-will, or any secret fear driving our decisions, bring it to the light of Your grace. Psalm one hundred and thirty-nine bids us pray: Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: and see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting. We submit our deepest motives to Your inspection. Do not let us be swayed by personal ambition, impatience, or the opinions of a culture that knows not Your ways. Shape our desires, Father, until our hearts beat in harmony with Yours. Grant us the wisdom of James chapter three — wisdom that is unhypocritical, steadfast, and fruitful in righteousness.

## Scene 5 — Waiting With Patience and Ordered Steps

The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD: though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for the LORD upholdeth him with his hand. What a profound comfort these words from Psalm thirty-seven are to our weary souls, Lord. There are times when waiting upon Your direction feels difficult, when the silence seems long, and when our hearts grow restless for an immediate answer. Grant us the grace of patience, Father. Teach us how to wait upon the Lord with a quiet spirit, knowing that he that believeth shall not make haste. 

If we have stumbled in our past choices, or if past missteps still cast a shadow of regret across our minds, remind us of Your great mercy. Your hand upholds us even when we falter; Your grace picks us up and sets our feet upon solid rock once more. We do not need to fear making a wrong turn when our hearts are genuinely surrendered to Your governance, for You are able to make all things work together for good to them that love You. Strengthen our courage as we wait. Let our souls rest secure in the knowledge that You are never late, never indifferent, and never mistaken. You are directing our steps right now, even when the fullness of Your plan is still unfolding before our eyes.

## Scene 6 — Committing the Way and Closing Benediction

Lord God Almighty, we now commit our way completely into Your hands, resting upon the promise of Proverbs sixteen: Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established. We release our burdens, our questions, and our timelines into Your care. We trust that because You have promised in Psalm thirty-2 verse eight to instruct us, teach us, and guide us with Your eye, You will faithfully perform what You have spoken. 

We go forth from this time of prayer with our faces turned toward the future, not with fear or trepidation, but with a quiet, holy confidence because You are with us. Let Your presence go before us in every task, every conversation, and every decision of the days to come. May Your peace guard our hearts, and may Your wisdom direct our hands.
