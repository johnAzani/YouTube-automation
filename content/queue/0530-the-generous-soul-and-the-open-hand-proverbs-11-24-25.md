## Scene 1 — Approaching Jehovah-Jireh at the Prepared Table

There are mornings when you look at what remains in the account or what is left on the shelf, and the calculation seems tight. You wonder how generosity is possible when every coin feels numbered and every demand is pressing in at once. But the Lord who prepares a table before us in the presence of our difficulties does not measure provision by the smallness of our storehouse. He measures it by the boundlessness of His own grace. We come before Him today to lay down our calculations, our anxieties about enough, and our quiet fears of scarcity, stepping into the rhythm of trust that He has ordained for His people.

Listen to the word of wisdom preserved in the eleventh chapter of Proverbs, verses twenty-four and twenty-five: There is that scattereth, and yet increaseth; and there is that withholdeth more than is meet, but it tendeth to poverty. The liberal soul shall be made fat: and he that watereth shall be watered also himself.

Lord God of our daily labour, we come acknowledging that everything we hold in our hands is first a gift from Your fingers. We confess how easily our hearts tighten when the margins grow narrow, how quickly we lean upon our own guarded strength rather than upon Your unfailing providence. Forgive us for treating our resources as if they were entirely our own creation. Open our eyes today to see that You are Jehovah-Jireh, the God who provides, and that You invite us to walk in the holy freedom of generous stewardship. Teach us to trust that when we honour You with our substance and extend kindness to those around us, we are planting seeds in soil that You have consecrated. Remove from us the spirit of fear that hoards and grips too tightly, and replace it with the quiet, unshakeable confidence that our cup runs over because Your hand is open wide toward us.

## Scene 2 — Open Doors & Heaven's Good Treasure Over Honest Labour

The economy of God confounds the wisdom of this world. While human prudence tells us to lock every door and keep every resource sealed away for safety alone, the scripture declares that scattering with a faithful heart leads to increase. Matthew Henry, in his commentary on these verses, observes that worldly selfishness defeats its own purpose, drying up the very streams it seeks to preserve, whereas the liberal soul — the open, generous heart — is watered by divine blessing just as it waters others. This is not a formula for manipulation or a bargain struck for personal gain; it is the natural character of those who live as stewards under an open heaven.

Father, we lift up our daily work, our vocations, and the labour of our hands before Your throne. Establish the work of our hands upon us, Lord; yea, the work of our hands establish Thou it. When we face closed doors or the weariness of toil that yields little apparent return, grant us the perseverance that comes from knowing You watch over the upright. We ask for open doors of honest opportunity — doors where we can serve our households, bless our neighbours, and exercise the talents You have deposited within us. Let no anxiety paralyze our hands. Where we have grown weary in doing good or hesitant to share what we have, renew our spirits. Let Your good treasure, the rain falling in its season upon our land and our labour, remind us that You are actively involved in the details of our daily sustenance.

## Scene 3 — Divine Wisdom, Discernment & Direction for Decisions

Stewardship requires wisdom at every turn. It is not merely about giving away what we have, but knowing how to manage, how to save, how to spend, and how to help with discernment and grace. Proverbs reminds us that wisdom is the principal thing, and in all our getting we are to get understanding. When we stand at crossroads regarding career choices, household budgeting, or helping those in need, we need more than human calculators; we need the counsel of the Spirit who searches all things.

Lord, grant us heavenly wisdom for the practical decisions that face us this week. When we do not know whether to hold fast or to release, give us the inner clarity that comes from Your Spirit. James reminds us that if any of us lack wisdom, we may ask of God, who giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not. We ask You now for that liberal wisdom. Guard our minds from impulsive decisions born out of panic, and keep us from the coldness of heart that ignores the needs right in front of us. Help us to manage our households with integrity, to pay what we owe, to honour our commitments, and to discern where You are calling us to invest our time, our energy, and our resources. Direct our steps, Lord, so that every choice we make honours Your name and preserves our peace.

## Scene 4 — Freedom From Anxious Toil & Contentment in Lean or Full Seasons

There is a heavy weight that settles upon the mind when we try to secure tomorrow entirely by our own strength. We calculate, we fret, we anticipate every possible shortfall, and in doing so, we eat the bread of sorrows. But the Master reminded His disciples not to be anxious about what they should eat or what they should wear, knowing that their Heavenly Father feeds the birds of the air and clothes the grass of the field. Contentment is not found in having an overflowing bank account; it is found in the deep, settled peace of resting in the care of the Shepherd.

Father, release us from the bondage of anxious toil. We cast our cares upon You because You care for us. Whether our current season feels full and abundant or lean and testing, teach us the secret of being content in whatever state we find ourselves. Remind us that our worth is not measured by our productivity or our possessions, but by our standing as Your children redeemed by grace. When the temptation arises to measure our security by numbers rather than by Your presence, gently draw our eyes back to Your faithfulness in days gone by. You have sustained us this far; You will not fail us now. Grant us rest tonight and steady calm tomorrow, knowing that our times are in Your hands.

## Scene 5 — Favour With God and Man & Generous Stewardship

The promise of our text is remarkably specific: he that watereth shall be watered also himself. The one who refreshes the dry places of another's life, who brings encouragement, practical help, or a comforting presence when others are parched, will find that God returns that refreshment into their own bosom. This is the divine circle of grace. Favour surrounds the righteous as a shield, opening hearts and smoothing paths in workplaces, communities, and families.

Lord God, we pray for Your divine favour to rest upon our lives and our relationships. Give us eyes to see the dryness in the lives of those around us — a coworker under pressure, a neighbor carrying a silent burden, a family member growing weary. Make us instruments of Your refreshment, not out of a sense of mere duty, but from hearts overflowing with Your love. And Lord, as we seek to be a blessing, we claim Your promise that we who water shall be watered also ourselves. When our own spirits feel depleted, send unexpected streams into our wilderness. Let grace abound toward us, that having all sufficiency in all things, we may abound to every good work. Let our homes be places of hospitality and quiet peace, reflecting the generosity of the table You have spread for us.

## Scene 6 — Overflowing Cup & Covenant Provision Benediction

As we close this time of prayer, we return to the sanctuary of Psalm twenty-three and the confidence of Philippians chapter four: my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus. The table is prepared. The oil of gladness is poured upon our heads. Our cup does not merely meet the rim; it overflows. We leave our petitions in the hands of Jehovah-Jireh, trusting Him to direct our work, guard our homes, and supply our daily bread.

The Lord bless thee, and keep thee: The Lord make His face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: The Lord lift up His countenance upon thee, and give thee peace. May goodness and mercy follow you through every day of your honest labour, and may your heart rest securely in the provision of the covenant-keeping God.
