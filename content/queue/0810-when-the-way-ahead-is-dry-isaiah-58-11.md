## Scene 1 — Bringing the Decision to the Lord

There are seasons when the road you thought you knew turns into dry ground, and every step requires a decision you feel unequipped to make. You wake up with a quiet weight in your chest, knowing that a choice is before you, but the horizon offers no clear landmarks. It is in moments like these, when your own understanding reaches its limit, that the voice of Scripture meets you right where you are. The prophet Isaiah records a promise that reaches across the centuries to speak directly into your uncertainty, offering not a formula for tomorrow, but a Person to lean upon today. Let us turn our hearts to the words of Isaiah chapter fifty-eight, verse eleven, which says: And the LORD shall guide thee continually, and satisfy thy soul in drought, and make fat thy bones: and thou shalt be like a watered garden, and like a spring of water, whose waters fail not. Here, the focus is drawn away from our frantic calculations and placed entirely upon the steady hand of God. We come before Him not because we have our plans neatly ordered, but precisely because we do not. We acknowledge that human wisdom is fragile, and we ask for the grace to trust a Guide who never loses His way. There is one more movement in this passage that invites us to look closer at what it means to be led by God in dry times.

## Scene 2 — Asking God for Wisdom

When we bring our unanswered questions before the Lord, James reminds us that if any lack wisdom, we may ask of God, who gives to all men liberally and upbraideth not. Matthew Henry, in his commentary on the Scriptures, notes that God's guidance is not a reluctant gift doled out to the wise, but a generous provision for those who humbly confess their need. In a dry season, when our inner resources are depleted and we cannot manufacture our own clarity, we are invited to ask for discernment without fear of being turned away. This asking is not a sign of failure; it is the first act of true trust. It is the moment we stop leaning on our own reasoning and open our hands to receive direction from above. God does not scold us for our confusion; He meets us in it with gentle patience. As we consider this, let us reflect on how our motives are aligned before Him, preparing our hearts to receive the lamp of His Word for the next step of the journey.

## Scene 3 — The Lamp of the Word

The guidance of the Lord is seldom a floodlight revealing the entire landscape from start to finish; rather, it is a faithful lamp to our feet, illuminating the immediate step we are called to take in obedience. Psalm one hundred nineteen reminds us of this steady light, and Isaiah fifty-eight points us to a deeper spiritual sustenance: the promise that God will satisfy the soul in drought. When decisions loom large and resources feel scarce, we look to the unchanging truth of Scripture rather than the shifting sands of circumstance. We do not need to know the outcome of every future month or year; we only need the grace for today and the assurance that His hand is holding ours. This is not a guarantee of worldly ease or a map to personal ambition, but a promise of divine presence when the ground is cracked and dry. Let us consider how godly counsel and honest self-examination help us walk steadily in the light of His Word.

## Scene 4 — Wise Counsel and Tested Motives

As we seek discernment, Scripture directs us to look outward as well as upward, recognizing the value of wise counsel. Proverbs reminds us that in the multitude of counsellors there is safety, and that plans are established when many advisers confer. True spiritual guidance in a major decision does not happen in isolation; it honors the community of faith and welcomes the sobering, loving insights of mature believers. Furthermore, seeking God's direction requires us to examine our own motives before Him, asking the Lord to search our hearts and try our thoughts. It must be said plainly that prayer walks alongside qualified medical, legal, financial, or other professional advice, and never replaces it. God uses practical wisdom, honest counsel, and sober judgment as instruments of His care. When we humble ourselves to listen to wise counsel alongside Scripture, our steps become firmer and our desires begin to align with His good will. There is yet another truth in our passage concerning the steady, patient unfolding of an ordered life.

## Scene 5 — Waiting With Patience and Ordered Steps

There are times when the hardest part of seeking guidance is simply waiting for the Lord's timing, holding fast to the promise that the steps of a good person are ordered by Him. When the path ahead feels shrouded in mist or when a long-awaited answer tarries, patience becomes the testing ground of faith. The prophet Isaiah speaks of a soul satisfied in drought and bones made strong, picturing an inner resilience that does not depend on outward comfort. Even when we stumble or feel weary, the Lord upholds us with His hand. We are reminded not to rush ahead of His cloud nor to lag behind in fear, but to rest in the reality that His continual guidance is active even in the quietest seasons of waiting. Let us now commit our specific decisions, our unanswered questions, and our daily routines into His keeping as we move toward the close of this prayer.

## Scene 6 — Committing the Way and Closing Benediction

Heavenly Father, we come before You acknowledging that we do not know what tomorrow brings, but we know the One who holds tomorrow in His hands. We thank You for Your promise that You will guide us continually, even when the road is dry and our own strength fails. We ask for a teachable heart, the wisdom to discern Your voice from the noise around us, and the courage to take the next faithful step in obedience. If there is someone listening right now carrying a heavy decision or standing at a crossroad where the way is unclear, draw near to them with Your peace and grant them discernment rooted in Your truth. Order our steps, satisfy our souls, and let our lives reflect Your sustaining grace.
