## Scene 1 — Bringing the Decision to the Lord

There are hours when the road ahead divides in two, and neither path bears a sign we can read in our own strength. You may be sitting quietly right now in a room where the air feels heavy with a choice that cannot wait much longer, wondering if you are about to turn left into regret or right into peace. The mind turns over the same questions until exhaustion settles behind your eyes, and the quiet prayer rises from deep within: Lord, which way? The voice of our own understanding offers arguments that pull in opposite directions, leaving us hesitant and weary. But the Scriptures remind us that we do not have to navigate the fog alone. Proverbs chapter three verses five and six tell us to trust in the Lord with all our hearts, and lean not unto our own understanding, acknowledging Him in all our ways so He may direct our paths. This is where every true step of guidance begins: not in knowing the end of the road from the start, but in turning to the One who made the journey and placing our uncertainty right into His hands. Let us bring this decision before Him now with an honest and quiet heart, laying down our anxiety and asking Him to quiet the noise of our own fears so we can hear His steady counsel.

## Scene 2 — Asking God for Wisdom

When we face a crossroads, the greatest need we carry is not merely for a favorable outcome, but for wisdom that comes from above. James chapter one verses five through six assures us that if any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not, and it shall be given him. But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. Matthew Henry, in his commentary on the Word, observes that God does not mock our honest perplexity; He welcomes our requests for discernment and grants His light freely to those who come with a teachable spirit. He does not reproach us for our confusion; rather, He invites us to look past our own shifting thoughts and anchor our minds in His unchanging truth. In moments of great decision, we often feel pressure to have every detail mapped out before we move, as if faith were a financial ledger where all columns must balance instantly. Yet the Lord trains our hearts to ask for the daily portion of discernment — just enough light for the step we are taking today. As we search the Scriptures, we find that God is far more interested in shaping our character through the choice than in turning our lives into a puzzle to be solved without trust. Let us ask Him now for that liberal measure of heavenly wisdom, recognizing that His ways are higher than our own and His timing is always perfect.

## Scene 3 — The Lamp of the Word

The human heart naturally desires a floodlight that illuminates every mile of the coming year, revealing every obstacle before our feet ever draw near to it. Yet the Lord in His tender mercy usually gives us something far more intimate: a lamp unto our feet and a light unto our path, as Psalm 119 verse 105 proclaims. A lamp does not show you the horizon; it shows you the ground directly beneath your next stride. In Isaiah chapter thirty verse twenty-one, the promise is given with exquisite tenderness: And thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left. Notice the intimacy of that picture. The voice does not shout from a distance; it speaks from behind us, keeping us from wandering off the narrow road, gently correcting our course when we incline toward our own stubborn preferences. We do not need to know what lies around the bend ten weeks from now; we only need to hear that quiet, inward confirmation of the Spirit agreeing with the written Word today. Let us quiet our souls before Him now, letting His Word wash away the frantic need for premature answers, and resting in the assurance that His guidance is faithful, patient, and close enough to touch.

## Scene 4 — Wise Counsel and Tested Motives

As we seek direction from the Lord, He rarely leaves us to our own isolated thoughts; He weaves the wisdom of godly counsel and honest self-examination into our process. Proverbs chapter fifteen verse twenty-two reminds us that without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established. When we are wrestling with a difficult decision, the temptation to consult only our own desires can run deep, filtering out any perspective that challenges what we already want to do. Therefore, we must bring our motives before the searchlight of God, praying with the psalmist in Psalm 139: Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: and see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting. It is also an act of faithful stewardship to recognize our own human limitations. Prayer and the counsel of Scripture walk hand in hand with wise, qualified professional advice — whether in matters of health, law, or daily provision — because God often uses the seasoned discernment of faithful believers and wise counselors to confirm the direction of His Spirit. Let us pause for a moment of quiet reflection, asking the Lord to purify our motives, to grant us the humility to listen to wise counsel, and to keep us from leaning upon our own hastily formed conclusions.

## Scene 5 — Waiting With Patience and Ordered Steps

There are seasons when the hardest part of guidance is not knowing what to do, but having to wait while the Lord prepares both the path and our hearts to walk upon it. Psalm 37 verse twenty-three tells us that the steps of a good man are ordered by the Lord: and he delighteth in his way. Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for the Lord upholdeth him with his hand. Notice that the text speaks of steps, one at a time, and acknowledges that even when we stumble or feel unsure, His hand remains beneath us. Waiting upon the Lord is never wasted time; it is the quiet forge where impatience is softened and trust is deepened. When we grow anxious because the door has not opened instantly, or because the clouds have not lifted from the valley, the Spirit reminds us to wait on the Lord: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart. Let us cast off the heavy burden of forcing outcomes before their time. Let us surrender our impatience to the God who numbers our days and directs our feet with unfailing wisdom. Take a slow, deep breath, and let His peace guard your mind against the panic of delay, knowing that the One who guides you has never lost His way.

## Scene 6 — Committing the Way and Closing Benediction

We have brought our uncertainty, our need for wisdom, our desire for His Word, and our patience to the throne of grace, and now we commit the entire matter into His faithful hands. Proverbs chapter sixteen verse three gives us the simple secret of peace: Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established. You do not have to carry the weight of this decision another moment; you can leave it here, knowing that He who hears your prayer is able to direct your path far better than you could ever chart it yourself.
