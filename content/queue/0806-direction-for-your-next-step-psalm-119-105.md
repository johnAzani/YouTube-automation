## Scene 1 — Bringing the Decision to the Lord

You are standing at a threshold, looking out into a landscape that feels unusually dark or uncertain. Perhaps it is a career shift, a relationship that requires a difficult choice, or simply the weight of an approaching deadline that feels heavier than the ones before it. In these moments, the natural inclination is to squint into the distance, trying to map out every mile of the journey before we have even taken the first step. But we know from Proverbs 3:5–6 that we are not called to map the entire geography of our lives; we are called to trust in the Lord with all our heart and lean not unto our own understanding. Psalm 25:4–5 invites us to ask, “Shew me thy ways, O LORD; teach me thy paths. Lead me in thy truth, and teach me: for thou art the God of my salvation; on thee do I wait all the day.” Bring that specific decision that has been weighing on your mind — the one that keeps you from resting in the present moment — and place it squarely before the Lord now. Do not try to solve it with your own logic; simply acknowledge that He is the architect of your steps and the light of your journey.

## Scene 2 — Asking God for Wisdom

There is a profound, quiet strength in admitting that we do not have the map. James 1:5 promises, “If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.” As Matthew Henry notes in his commentary, God’s giving is not just generous; it is a disposition of His character to provide for those who acknowledge their insufficiency. You do not need to possess all the wisdom of the world; you only need to possess the humility to ask the One who holds it. When we come to God, we are not coming to someone who grows impatient with our persistent questions or our hesitations. We come to a Father who is delighted to direct those who truly seek to be led. In this silence, ask Him for the wisdom to see the next step, not the final destination. Ask Him to quiet the noise of your own anxieties so that you might hear the gentle, steady leading of His spirit.

## Scene 3 — The Lamp of the Word

The verse before us today is one of the most comforting anchors in all of Scripture: “Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path” (Psalm 119:105). Notice the imagery: it is a lamp unto the feet, not a spotlight on the horizon. A lamp illuminates only what is immediately in front of us. It makes the next step clear and ensures that we do not stumble over the stones lying directly in our path. Charles Spurgeon, in The Treasury of David, remarks that the lamp is provided for our daily walk, keeping us from the snares that are laid in our way by the enemy. We often fear that God has hidden His plan from us, but the truth is that He is protecting us by only showing us the next faithful step. If He showed us the entire road, we would likely be overwhelmed by the distance or tempted to run ahead of His timing. Trust the light you have right now. Open the pages of the Bible and look for the principles that apply to your current challenge. The lamp is lit; you only need to look down and see the ground directly in front of you.

## Scene 4 — Wise Counsel & Tested Motives

Even with the light of Scripture, we are still communal beings. Proverbs 15:22 reminds us, “Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established.” God rarely guides us in isolation. He places people in our lives — mentors, elders, friends, and experts — to provide the wisdom we might be missing. If your decision involves technical, legal, financial, or medical complexities, remember that seeking professional counsel is a part of seeking God’s wisdom. God often speaks through the seasoned experience and objective perspectives of those He has placed around us. Furthermore, examine your heart as you seek this guidance. Psalm 139:23–24 asks, “Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.” Ask yourself if you are seeking God’s will, or if you are seeking a divine endorsement of what you have already decided to do. A teachable heart is the soil where God’s guidance grows best.

## Scene 5 — Waiting With Patience & Ordered Steps

The hardest part of guidance is often the waiting. We feel the pressure of time, and we fear that if we wait too long, the opportunity will pass or the situation will worsen. Yet, Psalm 37:23 assures us, “The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD: and he delighteth in his way.” Even when you feel you are standing still, God is ordering your steps. He is at work in the waiting, refining your character and preparing your heart for the change that is coming. Do not fear a wrong turn if your heart is truly submitted to Him. As Psalm 27:14 tells us, “Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart.” Even if you stumble, He is there to uphold you with His hand. You are not walking this path alone, and you are not walking it without a guide. The delay is not a denial; it is a preparation. Use this time of uncertainty to grow deeper in your prayer life, and let the quietness of your current season be a testament to your trust in His timing.

## Scene 6 — Committing the Way & Closing Benediction

We end this time of prayer by committing the outcome entirely to Him. Proverbs 16:3 says, “Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established.” When you release the need to control the final result, you free yourself to walk in His presence today. Rest in the knowledge that He who has led you this far will not abandon you now. His presence is the light that goes before you, and His peace is the rest that settles in your spirit. If there is a specific name, a specific need, or a specific fear you are carrying, bring it to the comments now; let this community stand with you in prayer as you navigate this season of life. We are seeking the same wisdom, the same lamp, and the same grace. May you walk out of this moment with a clearer view of the next step, resting in the absolute confidence that God is the author of your way.
