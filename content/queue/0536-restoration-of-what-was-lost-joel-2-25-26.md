## Scene 1 — The Table Prepared in the Wilderness of Memory

There are seasons when a man looks back across the span of his working years and sees only the empty places where harvests ought to have been. It may be a business that withered through no fault of dishonest dealing, a career derailed by shifting times, or simply the slow, grinding erosion of years spent in dry places where every effort felt like sowing seed upon stone. You sit with the ledger of your life, measuring the span of time that seems swallowed up by lean seasons, delayed doors, and hard trials, wondering if the years gone by are lost beyond recall. But the Lord God of harvest looks upon our years not as a ledger closed by human failure, but as a field subject to His sovereign mercy. In Joel chapter two, the prophet speaks to a people who had seen their fields stripped bare by the locust, the cankerworm, the caterpiller, and the palmerworm. Yet right in the middle of that desolation, the Lord turns to His people with a promise that defies human calculation. Let us lay our hands upon the table the Lord has prepared before us, bringing every regret over wasted time and delayed provision into His holy presence.

## Scene 2 — The Promise of Divine Restoration

We come before Jehovah-Jireh, not pleading our own perfection, but resting in the faithfulness of the God who redeems time itself. When the prophet declares the word of the Lord, He speaks of a restoration that goes far beyond mere replacement; it is the divine reconstitution of what was eaten away by trial and hardship. We read the sacred promise: And I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten, the cankerworm, and the caterpiller, and the palmerworm, my great army which I sent among you. Father, we acknowledge that You are the Lord of all our seasons, even those hard winters when the locusts of adversity stripped our labours bare. We do not ask for worldly vanity, but for the restoration of steady purpose, for the healing of our vocations, and for the opening of doors that no man can shut. As Matthew Henry notes in his commentary on these verses, God's grace can make up for the time lost in our wanderings and dry seasons, bringing a rich return where there seemed only barrenness. Grant us the grace to trust Your timing, knowing that Your hand is mighty to restore the strength of our hands and the fruit of our honest labour.

## Scene 3 — The Satisfaction of Daily Bread and Honest Labour

We pray for our daily work, our trades, our offices, and our households, asking for that settled peace which comes from honest labour blessed by heaven. The promise continues in the second verse of our reading: And ye shall eat in plenty, and be satisfied, and praise the name of the LORD your God, that hath dealt wondrously with you: and my people shall never be ashamed. Lord, we ask for our daily bread, for the quiet dignity of work that provides for our families and supports those in need. Where our hands have grown weary from prolonged toil and scant reward, pour out Your fresh strength. Give us wisdom in every business decision, integrity in every transaction, and favour with those who direct our paths. Let us know the steady satisfaction of work done well, free from the crushing weight of anxious debt and barren worry. Teach us to number our days and to commit the work of our hands unto You, that every task may be established by Your grace.

## Scene 4 — Wisdom and Guidance in Vocational Crossroad

In moments of career uncertainty or financial strain, the human heart easily leans toward fear or hasty schemes. We pray for divine discernment, that we may walk in the good works which God hath before ordained for us. Lord, when the path ahead seems narrow and the opportunities few, do not let us run ahead of Your counsel or lag behind Your leading. Grant us the spirit of wisdom, that we may recognize the open doors You set before us and pass through them with courage and calm assurance. Let Your word be a lamp unto our feet and a light unto our path through every vocational transition and seasonal adjustment. We cast off the heavy burden of trying to force our own way through closed doors, choosing instead to wait upon the Lord whose tender mercies fail not. Order our steps in Your truth, and establish the work of our hands in righteousness.

## Scene 5 — Favour, Integrity, and Generous Stewardship

We ask for Your favor to rest upon our daily endeavours — not for pride or selfish gain, but that we might be faithful stewards of Your bounty. Let integrity and uprightness preserve us in every workplace and marketplace. When You restore the years the locust has eaten, give us also a generous heart, that as our cup runs over, we may share our bread with the hungry and extend kindness to the stranger at our gate. Make us faithful in little things, knowing that he that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much. Guard our mouths from murmuring during the testing times, and fill our lips with thanksgiving for the small mercies and quiet daily provisions you shower upon us. Let our lives reflect the goodness of a God who deals wondrously with His servants, turning our shame into double honour.

## Scene 6 — Covenant Provision Benediction

Lord God of our days and our labour, we look unto You with quiet trust, knowing that our times are in Your hand. We rest in the promise that Your people shall never be ashamed, and we lean upon Your unfailing grace for all the days set before us. May goodness and mercy follow us through every remaining season of our lives, and may we dwell in the house of the Lord forever.
