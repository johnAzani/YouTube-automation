## Scene 1 — Entering His Gates With Thanksgiving

Grace and peace to you, and welcome to The Praise Garden. Wherever this moment finds you, whether the day has been kind or heavy, you are invited to stop for a few minutes and thank the Lord. Hear the King James Scripture in Psalm one hundred, verse four: Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.

The psalm pictures worshippers walking up to the temple, through the gates and into the courts. They did not arrive empty-handed, and they did not arrive silent. They came with thanks on their lips. Thanksgiving is not a fee we pay to be heard. It is simply the fitting way to come near to a God who has already been good to us. Father, as we come before You now, quiet our hurry and open our eyes to Your kindness.

## Scene 2 — Knowing Whose We Are

Before the psalm tells us to give thanks, it tells us why. Hear Psalm one hundred, verse three: Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.

Gratitude begins with remembering whose we are. We did not make ourselves. We did not choose the hour of our birth, the breath in our lungs, or the people who first cared for us. Every one of these was given. And the psalm adds something gentler still: we are the sheep of His pasture. A shepherd knows his flock, leads it to water, and goes looking for the one that wanders. Lord, thank You that we belong to You. Thank You for making us, for keeping us, and for seeking us when we strayed. Let that belonging be the first thing we thank You for today.

## Scene 3 — Naming His Goodness One by One

Thankfulness grows when it becomes specific. Hear Psalm one hundred seven, verse one: O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever. And verses eight and nine of the same psalm: Oh that men would praise the LORD for his goodness, and for his wonderful works to the children of men! For he satisfieth the longing soul, and filleth the hungry soul with goodness.

So let us name His goodness plainly. Lord, thank You for the food we have eaten and the water we have drunk. Thank You for a place to rest, for work to do, and for strength to do it. Thank You for a friend who called, for a kind word we did not expect, for a problem that turned out smaller than we feared. Thank You for mercies we never noticed at all. Listener, pause here and name one thing of your own before Him.

## Scene 4 — Thanksgiving When the Heart Is Heavy

Some who are listening do not feel thankful today, and Scripture does not ask you to pretend. Grief is not ingratitude. The same Bible that says give thanks also records tears, questions, and long nights. Hear First Thessalonians chapter five, verses sixteen through eighteen: Rejoice evermore. Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.

Notice the wording. It says in every thing, not for every thing. We are not asked to call a loss good. We are invited to find, even inside a hard season, that God is still present and still faithful. Merciful Father, for everyone who is weary, sorrowing, or afraid, be near. Where thanks feels impossible, receive a single honest sentence as worship. And where help is needed from a doctor, a counsellor, or a trusted friend, give courage to seek it.

## Scene 5 — Praise for Who God Is

Thanksgiving looks at what God has given. Praise lifts its eyes to who God is. Hear the closing verse, Psalm one hundred, verse five: For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations.

Three things are said of Him here. He is good, so nothing He does comes from malice. His mercy is everlasting, so it will not run out before we reach the end of our need. His truth endures to all generations, so the promise our grandparents trusted is the promise our children may trust. Lord, we praise You that You do not change with our moods or our circumstances. We praise You for Jesus Christ, in whom Your goodness, mercy, and truth came near to us. As Second Corinthians chapter nine, verse fifteen says: Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift.

## Scene 6 — A Grateful Step Into the Day

The psalm opens with a call that reaches every one of us. Psalm one hundred, verses one and two: Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands. Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing. Gladness in serving is where thanksgiving leads. So here is one small practice to carry with you: before this day closes, tell the Lord three things you are thankful for, and tell one person that you are thankful for them.

Heavenly Father, we have entered Your gates with thanksgiving, and we bless Your name. Keep a grateful heart alive in us through ordinary hours and difficult ones. Teach us to notice Your gifts, to share them freely, and to return to You with thanks again tomorrow. In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord. Type Amen to agree with this prayer, and subscribe to The Praise Garden to enter His gates with daily thanksgiving and praise.
