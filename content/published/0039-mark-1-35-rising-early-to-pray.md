## Scene 1 — Rising up a Great While Before Day (Mark 1:35)

And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.

The evening before this verse, Jesus had healed the sick until the whole city stood at His door. If anyone had earned a slow morning, it was Him. Yet Mark shows us the secret underneath the ministry: before the day took from Him, the Father filled Him. This is not a verse about admiring early risers. It is a window into how Jesus Himself began, and an invitation to begin the same way — not striving to impress God, but coming to receive from Him before we give to anyone else.

Lord Jesus, teach me Your morning. Before I check what the world wants from me, let me come to the Father who gives to me. I bring You my tired body, my full calendar and my honest heart. I do not rise early to be religious; I rise to be with You. Meet me in these first minutes, and let Your presence, not pressure, become the atmosphere of my whole day. Amen.

## Scene 2 — He Departed into a Solitary Place (Mark 1:35)

He went out, and departed into a solitary place.

Solitude, for Jesus, was never escape. It was alignment. Away from the crowds, away even from the grateful and the needy, He placed Himself where only the Father's voice defined the day. Matthew Henry observes that Christ's example commends secret prayer, especially in the morning, when the mind is fresh and the day is still ours to consecrate. Your solitary place may be small: a chair before the house wakes, a parked car, a quiet road. What makes it an altar is not the scenery. It is that God is met there on purpose, first.

Father, I set apart this place and this hour for You. Let the noise wait outside the door. Here, I am not a worker, not a parent, not a problem-solver; I am Your child, and that identity comes before every assignment. Speak into the decisions I cannot sort, the people I cannot fix, and the future I cannot see. Let this quiet not be empty, but full of You. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Scene 3 — And There Prayed (Mark 1:35)

And there prayed.

Three small words, and a whole life of power flows out of them. Jesus prayed not because He lacked relationship with the Father, but because He delighted in it and would not live a single day unconnected from it. If the Son of God began His mornings this way, how much more do we need to. Prayer at sunrise is where worry is exchanged for trust, where our plans are laid next to God's wisdom, and where the day is consecrated before it is spent. Nothing about this morning has to be carried alone, because it does not have to be carried first alone.

Heavenly Father, here I pray, before I perform. I lay before You the names on my heart, the work on my list and the battles I have been hiding. I exchange my anxiety for Your peace and my hurry for Your order. Let prayer not be the garnish on this day but its foundation. And when I rise from this place, let me rise sent, settled and strengthened by heaven. Amen.

## Scene 4 — All Men Seek for Thee (Mark 1:36-37)

And Simon and they that were with him followed after him. And when they had found him, they said unto him, All men seek for thee.

The demands found Him anyway. They always do. The beautiful thing is that when the crowd's urgency finally reached Jesus, it reached a man who had already been with the Father, so urgency did not get to lead. That is what morning prayer does for us. It does not remove the seeking, the ringing and the requesting from our day. It decides, in advance, who is in charge when they arrive. A person who has met God at sunrise can serve people all day without being owned by their expectations.

Lord, the demands of this day will seek for me, and I ask You to govern my answers before they ask. Give me grace to say yes where You are sending me and courage to say no where You are not. Let me serve my family, my work and my neighbours from fullness, not from frantic emptiness. Keep my heart soft, my priorities clean, and my pace set by Your Spirit rather than by every urgent voice. Amen.

## Scene 5 — Let Us Go: Sent into the Day (Mark 1:38)

And he said unto them, Let us go into the next towns, that I may preach there also: for therefore came I forth.

Out of the solitary place came clear purpose. Jesus did not stay in the quiet, however sweet it was, and He did not let yesterday's success choose today's assignment. He rose from prayer knowing why He had come forth, and He moved. That is the pattern for us: sunrise prayer is not a retreat from life but a commissioning into it. We go into our towns, our offices, our homes and our roads as people who know what we are for.

Father, having been with You, now send me into this day. Let us go: into the tasks that matter, the people who need encouragement, and the work You prepared for these hands. Establish what I build, bless those I serve, and keep me faithful in the small hidden obedience nobody applauds. Let my life today preach, in word and in kindness, the goodness I received in the quiet. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Scene 6 — Begin like Jesus: The Sending (Mark 1:35)

And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.

Tomorrow there will be another morning, and the invitation will still stand. But you do not have to wait for tomorrow to live from this one. You have heard the pattern of Jesus, you have prayed in His steps, and you go now as He went: from the Father's presence toward people, carrying heaven's peace into ordinary places. Keep this altar daily, and watch how a life is rebuilt one sunrise at a time.

Father, I go into this day the way Jesus began His: from prayer, in purpose, under Your hand. Bless my going out and my coming in. Let Your favour rest on my work, Your wisdom govern my words, and Your love be felt by everyone I meet. Until tomorrow's sunrise, keep me walking with You. In the mighty name of Jesus, amen.
