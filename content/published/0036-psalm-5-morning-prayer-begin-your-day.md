## Scene 1 — Give Ear to My Words, O Lord (Psalm 5:1–2)

Give ear to my words, O LORD, consider my meditation. Hearken unto the voice of my cry, my King, and my God: for unto thee will I pray.

Before this day says a single word to you, let your first word belong to God. Before messages, deadlines, and other people's urgencies begin naming your morning, come quietly to the altar and name the One who is already king over every hour ahead of you. You do not have to arrive with polished language. David brings God his meditation — even the groaning underneath his words — and trusts that heaven leans close enough to hear it.

Heavenly Father, this morning I give You my first attention and my honest heart. Consider not only the prayers I can phrase, but the hopes and heaviness I cannot yet explain. You are my King and my God, so I will not carry today as though I ruled it alone. Hear the voice of my cry, quiet the noise competing inside me, and let Your presence, not pressure, set the pace of everything that follows. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Scene 2 — In the Morning I Direct My Prayer to You (Psalm 5:3)

My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up.

There is a holy order in this verse. David does not drift into the day and hope prayer finds him later; he directs his prayer the way an archer aims an arrow — deliberately, before the battle, while the sky is still soft with first light. And then he looks up. Morning prayer is not only speaking to God; it is lifting your eyes in expectation that God will answer, lead, and go before you.

Lord, at this sunrise I direct my prayer to You on purpose. I lay before You the meetings I cannot control, the family members I am concerned for, the decisions waiting on my desk, and the private battles nobody else can see. I will not merely worry about them in Your direction; I place them in Your hands. Now teach me to look up — away from fear, away from yesterday, and toward the faithfulness of the God who has never once been late to His own morning. Amen.

## Scene 3 — You Are Not a God Who Has Pleasure in Wickedness (Psalm 5:4–6)

For thou art not a God that hath pleasure in wickedness: neither shall evil dwell with thee. The foolish shall not stand in thy sight: thou hatest all workers of iniquity. Thou shalt destroy them that speak leasing: the LORD will abhor the bloody and deceitful man.

The morning altar is also a place of cleansing. The God who meets us at sunrise is tender, but He is never careless about evil. So before we ask Him to order our steps, we let His holiness search our own hearts. This is not shame; it is mercy arriving early, clearing the ground before the day is built on it.

Search me this morning, O God, and show me anything in me that does not belong in Your presence — a careless word I need to make right, a hidden compromise I have learned to excuse, a lie fear has been telling me until I almost believed it. I renounce deceit, bitterness, and every quiet agreement with darkness. Wash my motives, straighten my speech, and let my life today be a dwelling place fit for Your Spirit. Lead me out of every crooked path and into clean, honest light. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Scene 4 — Lead Me, O Lord, in Your Righteousness (Psalm 5:8)

Lead me, O LORD, in thy righteousness because of mine enemies; make thy way straight before my face.

David does not pray, "Lord, remove every difficult person and every narrow place," though God could. He prays something stronger: lead *me*. Make Your way straight before my face. Some of today's opposition will not move, some conversations will still be hard, and some answers will not arrive by noon. But a person led by God can walk through a complicated day without becoming crooked inside it.

Righteous Lord, I ask You to lead me today in Your righteousness. When I am tempted to react in anger, make Your way straight. When I do not know which opportunity is Yours and which is only noise, make Your way straight. When enemies — seen or unseen — would love to pull me into distraction, offence, or fear, keep my feet on Your path. Let my character stay clean under pressure, my decisions stay honest in private, and my words carry Your wisdom into every room I enter. Go before me, Lord, and I will follow. Amen.

## Scene 5 — Let All Who Put Their Trust in You Rejoice (Psalm 5:11)

But let all those that put their trust in thee rejoice: let them ever shout for joy, because thou defendest them: let them also that love thy name be joyful in thee.

Notice that the joy in this psalm is not reserved for people whose problems have already disappeared. It belongs to those who put their trust in God while He is defending them. Joy, then, is not the sound of a finished battle; it is the sound of a guarded heart. Before you know how today will turn out, heaven invites you to rejoice in who is standing over you while it unfolds.

Father, this morning I choose trust, and with trust I choose joy. I will not wait for perfect conditions before I praise You. You defend me, so I can breathe again. You are over my family, so I can release them into Your keeping. You love Your name in me, so I can face this day without dread as my uniform. Let a quiet shout of joy rise in my spirit — not denial, not noise, but confidence that the God who began this morning with me will still be God at midnight. I rejoice in You, Lord. Amen.

## Scene 6 — You Will Bless the Righteous and Shield Me with Favour (Psalm 5:12)

For thou, LORD, wilt bless the righteous; with favour wilt thou compass him as with a shield.

This is the declaration to carry out of the door. A shield does not wait behind you, remembering yesterday's blows; a shield moves with you, facing what you will face. David says God's favour will *compass* you — surround you on every side — as you walk into ordinary places: the road, the office, the market, the hospital corridor, the classroom, the home that needs your patience.

Lord, I receive Your blessing over this day. Compass me with favour as with a shield — before me in the way I should go, behind me in mercy over what is already done, and around my mind when anxious thoughts try to gather. Bless the work of my hands, guard the people I love, and let Your favour open only the doors that lead to life. I begin this day at Your altar, and I will walk through it under Your shield. In the mighty name of Jesus, amen. Go in peace — and come back tomorrow morning. The altar will be here, and so will He.
