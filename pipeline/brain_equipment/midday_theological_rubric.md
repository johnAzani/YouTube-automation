# Midday Watch Theological & Scriptural Exposition Rubric

## Script Structure (The 6-Movement Noonday Journey)
Every long-form midday script (target duration 5:30 – 6:30 minutes; at least the production word floor of narration across the scenes) must follow a structured 6-movement devotional arc that moves from stepping out of the day's noise to being sent back into it:

### Movement 1: The Noonday Hook & The Pause
- **Opening Line:** An immediate invitation to stop, mid-task, and stand before God — the Psalm 55:17 posture: at noon I will pray, and cry aloud, and he shall hear my voice.
- **Verse Recitation:** The opening verse of the chapter/passage.
- **Exposition:** Prayer is not only for the edges of the day. The middle hour, when strength is most spent and the work most demanding, is a place of meeting, not a gap to be endured.

### Movement 2: Light at Noon & The Noonday Shadow
- **Thematic Focus:** Psalm 91:6 names "the destruction that wasteth at noonday"; the middle of the day has its own hazards — heat, haste, sudden bad news. God is present in both the light and the shadow it casts.
- **Verse Recitation:** Second key verse/section.
- **Exposition:** The noonday threat is the threat that arrives when we are least watchful. Draw on Matthew Henry on divine protection that does not require the danger to be visible to be real.

### Movement 3: The Work of the Hands
- **Thematic Focus:** The afternoon's labour, decisions, deadlines, difficult conversations and unfinished duty are committed to God — Proverbs 16:3: "Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established."
- **Verse Recitation:** Third key verse/section.
- **Exposition:** God is not only interested in the moments set aside for Him. Draw on Spurgeon on committing the whole enterprise — method, motive and outcome — rather than merely asking for success.

### Movement 4: Strength for the Hours That Remain
- **Thematic Focus:** Isaiah 40:31: those who wait upon the Lord renew their strength. The afternoon is not survived by willpower but by renewal.
- **Verse Recitation:** Fourth key verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Waiting is an active posture — the mid-shift turning of the heart. Renewal is given, not manufactured; the script must not turn it into a productivity technique.

### Movement 5: Integrity in the Middle
- **Thematic Focus:** The temptation of midday is the shortcut: the half-truth, the cut corner, the temper that flares when no one is watching. The prayer asks for a clean heart in unglamorous hours.
- **Verse Recitation:** Fifth key verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Character is what a person is at noon, when the morning's resolve and the evening's accounting are both absent. Draw on Matthew Henry on integrity as the fruit of fellowship with God, not of reputation.

### Movement 6: The Sending Back Into the Day
- **Thematic Focus:** The listener is sent back to the desk, the field, the kitchen or the road — not away from the day but into it, carrying the peace of God.
- **Verse Recitation:** Closing benediction verse.
- **Exposition:** Close with a blessing over the hours that remain and this exact call-to-action: "Type Amen to agree with this prayer, and subscribe to The Midday Watch to pause and pray with us at noon."

## Theological Boundaries
- KJV quotations only, checked verbatim. Never paraphrase scripture inside quotation marks.
- Attribute every commentator cited (Spurgeon, Matthew Henry) to the work it comes from; never invent a quotation. If a claim cannot be attributed, state it as the script's own exposition instead.
- No date-setting, no predictions, no prosperity promises, no claims about named individuals or current events.
- Every prayer request referenced is held in general terms; never imply a pastoral follow-up that the automation cannot deliver.
