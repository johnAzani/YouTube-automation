# Praise Garden Theological & Scriptural Exposition Rubric

## Script Structure (The 6-Movement Thanksgiving & Praise Journey)
Every long-form Praise Garden script (target duration 5:30 – 6:30 minutes; at least the production word floor of narration across the scenes) must follow a structured 6-movement devotional arc. Vary wording, imagery, and pacing to fit the passage; the arc is a shape, not a template to refill.

### Movement 1: Entering His Gates With Thanksgiving
- **Thematic Focus:** Psalm 100:1–5 & Psalm 95:1–2 — "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise."
- **Verse Recitation:** Opening KJV verse of the passage.
- **Exposition:** Open with an immediate, specific invitation to pause and thank God, and establish from Scripture that thanksgiving is the way into His presence because the LORD is good and His mercy is everlasting.

### Movement 2: Remembering His Benefits
- **Thematic Focus:** Psalm 103:1–5 & Psalm 107:1–9 — "Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits."
- **Verse Recitation:** Second key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Draw on Charles Spurgeon (*The Treasury of David*) or Matthew Henry (*Commentary on the Whole Bible*) to name God's mercies one by one — forgiveness, compassion, daily strength — so that gratitude is concrete rather than vague.

### Movement 3: Honest Praise in Hard Seasons
- **Thematic Focus:** Habakkuk 3:17–19, Psalm 42:11 & Lamentations 3:22–23 — "Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation."
- **Verse Recitation:** Third key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Acknowledge loss, grief, and unanswered questions plainly. Show that the prophet praised without pretending the fig tree had blossomed; never shame the listener who finds thanksgiving difficult.

### Movement 4: Thanksgiving in Prayer
- **Thematic Focus:** Philippians 4:6–7, 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18 & Colossians 3:15–17 — "In every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God."
- **Verse Recitation:** Fourth key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Lead the listener to bring real requests alongside thanks, receiving the peace of God. Do not treat prayer as a replacement for medical or mental-health care.

### Movement 5: Worship for Who God Is
- **Thematic Focus:** Psalm 145:1–9, Psalm 63:3–4 & Psalm 150 — "Great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised; and his greatness is unsearchable."
- **Verse Recitation:** Fifth key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Turn from the gifts to the Giver: praise the character of God — gracious, full of compassion, slow to anger — and thank Him above all for Christ, His unspeakable gift (2 Corinthians 9:15).

### Movement 6: A Life of Thanks & Closing Doxology
- **Thematic Focus:** Hebrews 13:15, Psalm 118:24 & Revelation 7:12 — "Blessing, and glory, and wisdom, and thanksgiving, and honour, and power, and might, be unto our God for ever and ever. Amen."
- **Verse Recitation:** Closing KJV benediction verse.
- **Exposition:** Send the listener back into the day with one simple, practical habit of gratitude and seal the prayer in doxology. End with this exact call-to-action: "Type Amen to agree with this prayer, and subscribe to The Praise Garden to enter His gates with daily thanksgiving and praise."

## Theological & Pastoral Boundaries
- KJV quotations only, checked verbatim. Never paraphrase scripture inside quotation marks.
- Attribute every commentator cited (Spurgeon, Matthew Henry) to the work it comes from; never invent a quotation. If a claim cannot be attributed, state it as the script's own exposition instead.
- Identify the speaker, audience, and setting of the passage where relevant, and keep the application faithful to that context.
- Gratitude is never a transaction: never promise guaranteed financial, physical, or emotional outcomes in return for praise.
- Never pressure or shame listeners who are grieving; lament and thanksgiving stand together in Scripture.
- Never discourage professional support or medical care.
- Every prayer request referenced is held in general pastoral terms; never imply individual follow-up that the automation cannot deliver.
