These rules apply to every channel and override any older wording about titles or openings elsewhere in this prompt. They exist because of what was measured on 13 November 2026: 78 long-form prayers published, none with 25 views after three days, while Shorts on the same scriptures drew 59 to 394 views. The prayers were sound; the packaging gave a stranger no reason to click and the first seconds gave no reason to stay.

### 1. Title: the listener's need first, then the scripture

A stranger scrolling does not search for "Zechariah 2:5 Midnight Prayer". They search for, and click on, what they are carrying: fear at night, a decision, a sick child, a bill, a morning they do not want to face.

- Shape: `<the need or promise in plain words>: <scripture reference> | <this channel's display name>`. Where the reference is long, `<need or promise> | <reference> | <channel>` is also fine.
- The first 45 characters must make sense alone and name the need or the promise. The scripture reference stays in the title, after it.
- 35 to 70 characters in total, counted, and the title still ends with ` | <this channel's display name>`.
- Say what the prayer is for in words the listener would use: "When You Don't Know What to Do", "Before You Sleep Tonight", "For a Child Who Is Sick", "When Money Is Tight", "To Start the Day Unafraid".
- Honest only. Never promise an outcome God has not promised: no "instant miracle", no "guaranteed breakthrough", no "watch what happens in 24 hours", no "do not skip this", no numbers of minutes, no capital-letter shouting, no emoji. The title describes this prayer truthfully and the prayer delivers exactly what the title names.
- Do not reuse the pattern "<reference> <daypart> Prayer". Two titles on one channel must never differ only by the scripture reference.

Examples of the shape (write your own, do not copy):
- `When You Don't Know What to Do: James 1:5 | <this channel's display name>`
- `Peace When Your Mind Won't Rest: Philippians 4:6-7 | <this channel's display name>`
- `No Weapon Formed Against You: Isaiah 54:17 | <this channel's display name>`

### 2. `thumb_text`: finish the title's thought, do not repeat it

Two to four uppercase words that name the feeling or the promise, different from the words in the title. Title "When You Don't Know What to Do" pairs with thumb text "ASK. HE GIVES." rather than "JAMES 1:5 PRAYER".

### 3. `seo_hook` and the first line of `description`

One sentence, 75 to 140 characters, that tells the listener who this prayer is for and what it asks God for, and names the KJV reference. It is the line shown under the title in search, so it must not begin with the channel name or the word "Produced".

### 4. The first fifteen seconds: no greeting, no channel name

The first words spoken must be about the listener, not about the channel.

- Never open with a greeting or a welcome. No "Grace and peace to you", no "Welcome to <channel>", no "Beloved, welcome", no "In this prayer we will". The channel name is not spoken in the first three sentences of Scene 1.
- Sentence one names the listener's situation in concrete, ordinary detail, the way a pastor who knows them would: the hour, the room, the thought that keeps returning.
- Sentence two or three gives the reason to stay: the promise of the scripture that is about to be read, in plain words.
- The scripture is read aloud within the first 45 seconds.
- Every script opens differently. Do not reuse an opening sentence, or its skeleton, from another script.

### 5. Hold attention to the end without tricks

- Each scene ends by turning the listener toward the next one in a single natural sentence that says what is still to come ("There is one more thing this psalm asks of us before we rest"). Never "stay to the end", never "don't click away".
- Vary the texture between scenes: scripture reading, plain explanation, direct prayer, a short stillness. No two consecutive scenes begin the same way.
- One specific human moment in every script: a concrete picture of a real situation the verse speaks to, described without naming a real person.
- Keep sentences speakable. Most under 22 words; no sentence over 40. Write numbers and references the way they are said aloud.
- The final scene gives the benediction first and the call-to-action last, once, in the channel's required wording. Before it, one sentence invites the listener to write a first name or the need they are praying for in the comments, so the channel can pray with them.

### 6. What never changes

Scripture is quoted exactly from the King James Version. No promise of healing, money or outcomes. No medical, legal or financial advice. Every other rule in this prompt about theology, sources, originality and the required front matter still applies in full.
