# Healing Balm Theological & Scriptural Exposition Rubric

## Script Structure (The 6-Movement Balm of Gilead Restoration Journey)
Every long-form Healing Balm script (target duration 5:30 – 6:30 minutes; at least the production word floor of narration across the scenes) must follow a structured 6-movement devotional arc that brings the weary, recovering, or anxious listener before the Great Physician in KJV scripture and compassionate intercession:

### Movement 1: Approaching the Great Physician in Faith
- **Opening Line:** An immediate, compassionate invitation to bring every physical ache, anxious thought, weary season, or loved one in need of recovery unto the throne of grace (Hebrews 4:16).
- **Verse Recitation:** Opening KJV verse of the passage.
- **Exposition:** Introduce the covenant mercy of Jehovah-Rapha ("I am the LORD that healeth thee," Exodus 15:26), assuring the listener that no tear or quiet sigh goes unseen by our Father (Psalm 56:8).

### Movement 2: The Balm of Gilead for Body, Soul & Spirit
- **Thematic Focus:** Jeremiah 30:17 & Psalm 103:2–4 — "Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases; Who redeemeth thy life from destruction."
- **Verse Recitation:** Second key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Draw on Charles Spurgeon (*The Treasury of David*) or Matthew Henry (*Commentary on the Whole Bible*) to unfold how Christ ministers both inward pardon and tender compassion toward human frailty.

### Movement 3: Binding Up the Brokenhearted & Quieting Anxious Minds
- **Thematic Focus:** Psalm 147:3, Philippians 4:6–7 & Isaiah 26:3 — "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee."
- **Verse Recitation:** Third key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Intercede specifically over heavy grief, emotional exhaustion, fearful waiting for news, and restless thoughts, asking the Prince of Peace to garrison heart and mind in Christ Jesus.

### Movement 4: Strength in Weakness & Sustaining Grace for Today
- **Thematic Focus:** Isaiah 40:29–31 & 2 Corinthians 12:9 — "My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness."
- **Verse Recitation:** Fourth key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Cite Spurgeon or Matthew Henry on waiting upon the Lord for renewed strength — encouraging those walking through prolonged recovery, hospital stays, or chronic weariness that Christ's sustaining grace never runs dry.

### Movement 5: Wisdom Over Caregivers, Physicians & Loved Ones
- **Thematic Focus:** Psalm 41:3, James 5:14–16 & Colossians 4:14 — praying over sick loved ones, hospital rooms, families standing watch, and skilled wisdom for physicians, nurses, and caregivers.
- **Verse Recitation:** Fifth key KJV verse/section.
- **Exposition:** Lift up both the one in need of restoration and those caring for them, asking the Lord to guide every hand, steady every household, and fill every room with quiet hope.

### Movement 6: Covenant Restoration & Peace Benediction
- **Thematic Focus:** Malachi 4:2 & 3 John 1:2 — the Sun of Righteousness arising with healing in His wings and peace resting upon body and soul.
- **Verse Recitation:** Closing KJV benediction verse.
- **Exposition:** Close with a gentle pastoral benediction and this exact call-to-action: "Type Amen to agree with this prayer, and subscribe to The Healing Balm to receive God's healing peace in daily prayer."

## Theological & Safety Boundaries
- KJV quotations only, checked verbatim. Never paraphrase scripture inside quotation marks.
- Attribute every commentator cited (Spurgeon, Matthew Henry) to the work it comes from; never invent a quotation. If a claim cannot be attributed, state it as the script's own exposition instead.
- Never issue medical advice, never promise instant cessation of prescribed treatment, and never blame sickness on a lack of faith.
- Every prayer request referenced is held in general pastoral terms; never imply individual follow-up that the automation cannot deliver.
